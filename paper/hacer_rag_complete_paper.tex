\begin{abstract}
On-device Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) enables privacy-preserving, low-latency question answering on resource-constrained mobile hardware. However, executing autoregressive Small Language Models (SLMs) on mobile CPUs incurs severe prompt-prefill latency when repeatedly encoding retrieved context. While precomputing and restoring Key-Value (KV) cache prefixes can eliminate redundant prefill computation, existing mobile runtimes treat KV-cache files as opaque conversational histories. This introduces a critical systems failure mode: \emph{silent stale-cache hallucination}, where outdated or revoked retrieved evidence preserved in serialized KV caches causes the model to generate factually incorrect responses without detection. Furthermore, dynamic context-capacity constraints and continuation-token boundary effects frequently trigger out-of-memory (OOM) aborts or silent truncation on memory-limited handsets. In this paper, we present \systemname{} (\emph{Hierarchical Adaptive Cache, Evidence-version-aware Repair for on-device RAG}), a mobile inference system designed for reliable and efficient local RAG on commodity Android smartphones. \systemname{} pairs a canonical evidence layout with cryptographic chunk-level hashing to bind every KV cache segment to an explicit evidence version. When retrieved context changes, \systemname{} executes a selective repair policy that preserves valid prefix KV tensors while triggering online prefill only for updated downstream chunks, falling back safely to clean scratch prefill under cache corruption. In addition, \systemname{} introduces a JNI-level context-budget safety guard that dynamically enforces headroom constraints and models continuation boundary-token alignment. We evaluate \systemname{} across two physical Android hardware tiers: a mid-range baseline (Xiaomi Redmi 12 5G, Qualcomm Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, 4~GB RAM, UFS 2.2, Android 14) and a flagship tier (Samsung Galaxy S25+, Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite, 12~GB RAM, UFS 4.0, Android 16) using a 4-bit quantized Gemma~2~2B model executing via \texttt{llama.rn} and \texttt{llama.cpp}. In a 90-matched-pair empirical scaling study across nine configurations ($K \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ chunks, $n_{\text{ctx}} \in \{512, 1024, 2048\}$), \systemname{} achieves mean prompt-prefill compute reductions of 19.34--21.21\% for $K=1$ (84 cached tokens), 38.53--40.37\% for $K=2$ (158 cached tokens), and 86.06--86.67\% for $K=4$ (341 cached tokens) on the mid-range device, saving up to 47.33~s of CPU execution and delivering up to $3.65\times$ end-to-end wall speedup with zero native truncation and 100\% deterministic output parity. On the flagship device, despite a $4.04\times$ raw compute speedup, relative prefill compute reductions remain invariant (16.35\% at $K=1$, 36.73\% at $K=2$, and 85.23\% at $K=4$, a delta of only 1.08\% at $K=4$). Balanced Two-Way ANOVAs on both platforms confirm that prefill reductions are governed by cached token count ($p < 10^{-15}$), with no statistically significant main effect of context capacity ($p = 0.449$ on Device A; $p = 0.650$ on Device B) or interaction ($p > 0.72$). Physical storage profiling demonstrates that UFS 4.0 flash loads cached sessions in only 35.6--56.2~ms while saving 10.8--11.5~s of CPU prefill, establishing a storage-to-compute speedup multiplier of $204\times\text{--}229\times$. In repeated multi-chunk RAG trials, \systemname{} achieves 100\% multi-hop factual accuracy across both platforms. Across five dynamic corpus failure scenarios (content updates, document revocation, chunk reordering, partial updates, and cache corruption), \systemname{} eliminates the stale-cache hallucinations and runtime crashes where naive session reuse fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Executing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) locally on mobile devices offers compelling advantages for personal privacy, offline capability, and predictable service availability~\cite{lewis2020retrieval,wu2024mobilellm}. In on-device RAG pipelines, an on-device retriever (e.g., SQLite BM25 or mobile vector indices) extracts relevant evidence chunks from a local knowledge store, constructs an augmented prompt, and feeds it into an on-device Small Language Model (SLM) for autoregressive generation~\cite{guu2020realm,liu2026percache}.

However, modern mobile devices, spanning both entry-level handsets and high-end flagship smartphones, face severe hardware and architectural constraints. Handsets in the high-volume budget tier commonly feature heterogeneous octa-core SoCs (such as the Qualcomm Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, equipped with $2\times$ Cortex-A78 performance cores and $6\times$ Cortex-A55 efficiency cores) paired with 4--6~GB of shared LPDDR4X memory~\cite{reddi2021mlperfmobile}. Even premium flagship devices (such as the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite with custom Oryon cores and 12~GB LPDDR5X memory) face thermal dissipation envelopes and battery power constraints. When running 2-billion to 4-billion parameter language models quantized to 4-bit integers (e.g., Gemma~2~2B Instruct Q4\_K\_M~\cite{gemma2024gemma2}), the inference latency is sharply bimodal:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Autoregressive Decode Phase:} Generating output tokens proceeds sequentially at approximately 20--30~tokens per second on budget cores (and 60--80~tokens/s on flagship cores), bound primarily by memory bandwidth and per-token vector-matrix products.
    \item \textbf{Prompt Prefill Phase:} Ingesting the concatenated prompt (system instructions, multiple retrieved document chunks, conversation history, and user query) requires dense matrix-matrix evaluations across all transformer layers. On CPU-bound mobile SoCs, prefilling a 400-token prompt requires 50--65~seconds of sustained computation on mid-range hardware and 12--14~seconds on flagship cores, dominating end-to-end response time.
\end{enumerate}

When users issue multiple queries against the same local document corpus or personal workspace, the retriever repeatedly selects identical or partially overlapping document chunks~\cite{lu2024turborag,liu2026percache}. In traditional inference runtimes, the mobile CPU is forced to re-encode these static chunks on every interaction, dissipating scarce battery capacity and inducing thermal throttling.

While caching precomputed Key-Value (KV) tensors appears to be a natural optimization, saving and restoring KV caches on mobile platforms introduces major system challenges that existing LLM engines do not solve. In particular, existing mobile runtimes such as \texttt{llama.rn} and \texttt{llama.cpp}~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp} treat KV-cache files as opaque session snapshots designed for conversational turn resumption. Applying these session primitives blindly to on-device RAG exposes three critical vulnerabilities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Silent Stale-KV Hallucination:} If a local document is edited, updated, or revoked (e.g., a corporate travel policy, personal medical record, or pricing schedule), naive session restoration reloads outdated KV tensors into the model's resident attention memory. Because the native engine cannot distinguish fresh evidence from stale cache bytes, the model generates hallucinated, obsolete facts with high confidence, even though the on-disk document index has been updated.
    \item \textbf{Context Headroom Violations:} Allocating static context buffers on low-RAM handsets risks triggering silent engine truncations or Out-Of-Memory (OOM) process termination by the Android Low Memory Killer (LMK). Mobile RAG runtimes lack dynamic context budgeting that reconciles native engine limits with variable prompt and generation budgets.
    \item \textbf{Continuation Boundary Misalignment:} Concatenating variable query text to a precomputed KV cache prefix without accounting for tokenizer boundary-merging behaviors causes native runtime index misalignments, producing garbled generation or redundant token re-evaluation.
\end{itemize}

To address these challenges, we present \systemname{} (\emph{Hierarchical Adaptive Cache, Evidence-version-aware Repair for on-device RAG}). \systemname{} is an on-device RAG runtime architecture that transforms raw KV-cache serialization into an evidence-version-aware, context-safe, and selectively repairable caching system for commodity mobile devices.

\section{Motivation and Problem Statement}
\label{sec:motivation}

\subsection{Mobile Execution Bottlenecks}
On commodity mobile devices, CPU inference execution time is heavily dominated by prompt prefill. On our physical Android baseline testbed (Qualcomm Snapdragon 4 Gen 2), prefilling a 395-token prompt using 4 threads takes an average of 54.2~seconds, corresponding to an effective prefill throughput of only 7.3~tokens/second. In contrast, reading a 40~MB serialized KV cache from the device's UFS 2.2 physical flash storage requires less than 350~milliseconds. Even on our evaluated flagship device (Snapdragon 8 Elite with UFS 4.0), prefilling the prompt takes 13.1~seconds, while reading the session binary from UFS 4.0 flash takes only 48.0~milliseconds. This fundamental asymmetry, in which CPU computation is $150\times$ to $229\times$ slower than flash I/O, creates an enormous theoretical opportunity for KV-cache reuse on mobile platforms.

\subsection{The Stale-KV Hallucination Problem}
In server-class deployments, KV-cache management systems such as vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} manage in-memory paged caches across persistent worker pools. On mobile devices, however, volatile RAM is severely constrained (4~GB total system RAM shared between the OS, UI, display compositor, and background services on budget devices). Consequently, KV caches must be persisted to non-volatile flash storage between interactions.

Existing mobile LLM wrappers (such as \texttt{llama.rn}) expose file-level session persistence (\texttt{saveSession} and \texttt{loadSession}). However, these APIs operate blindly: they associate a cache file with an arbitrary file path or session identifier, without any verification of semantic validity or document freshness. If a user modifies a note or an enterprise document is synchronized in the background, a subsequent query that blindly reloads the cached session will read stale KV representations into the attention layers. The LLM will attend to the old Key-Value vectors and output obsolete facts, a failure mode we term \emph{Silent Stale-KV Hallucination}.

\subsection{Context Budgeting and Engine Headroom}
Mobile LLM inference runtimes allocate fixed-size contiguous KV-cache arenas upon initialization to prevent memory fragmentation. For example, an allocated context window of $n_{\text{ctx}}$ tokens requires reserving memory for $2 \times L \times H \times D \times n_{\text{ctx}}$ elements, where $L$ is the layer count, $H$ is the head count, and $D$ is the head dimension. On memory-constrained devices, developers frequently set $n_{\text{ctx}}$ to small values (e.g., 512 or 1024 tokens) to fit within Android process limits.

However, native engines such as \texttt{llama.cpp} enforce strict internal reserved margins (e.g., 4 tokens reserved for internal control and EOS markers). If the combined length of the precomputed prefix, continuation query, and requested autoregressive generation tokens exceeds this boundary, the engine silently drops leading context tokens without raising an error. This silent truncation alters the attention mask and corrupts prompt semantics. A mobile RAG system must therefore enforce verifiable, JNI-level context-budget safety before initiating inference.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background_related}

\subsection{Transformer KV-Cache Fundamentals}
Autoregressive Transformer language models process input tokens by projecting hidden states $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}$ at each layer $l \in [1, L]$ into Query ($Q_l$), Key ($K_l$), and Value ($V_l$) matrices via weight projections $W_Q, W_K, W_V \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$:
\begin{equation}
    Q_l = X W_Q, \quad K_l = X W_K, \quad V_l = X W_V.
\end{equation}
In decoder-only architectures, a strictly lower-triangular causal attention mask $M \in \{0, -\infty\}^{T \times T}$ ensures that token $t$ attends only to tokens at positions $\tau \le t$. The scaled dot-product attention $\operatorname{Attn}(\cdot)$ is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Attn}(Q_l, K_l, V_l) = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_l K_l^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}} + M\right) V_l.
\end{equation}
Because the attention mask is strictly causal, the Key and Value representations computed for any prefix of length $P < T$ are mathematically independent of subsequent tokens $t > P$. When extending a prompt prefix $X_{1:P}$ with continuation tokens $X_{P+1:T}$, the prefix Key-Value tensors $K_{l, 1:P}$ and $V_{l, 1:P}$ can be preserved and reused directly without re-evaluation, provided the positional encodings and model weights remain identical.

In mobile inference runtimes such as \texttt{llama.cpp}~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp}, serialized session files record the internal state of the KV arena along with the evaluated token sequence. Restoring a session reloads these tensors into the engine's memory arena, allowing the prefill phase for the continuation to begin at position $P+1$.

\subsection{Prior Work on KV-Cache Reuse in RAG}
Recent literature has explored KV-cache reuse in retrieval-augmented workflows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{TurboRAG~\cite{lu2024turborag}:} Precomputes document KV caches offline and retrieves them at query time. However, TurboRAG requires specialized positional embedding transformations and optional model fine-tuning to preserve cross-attention coherence when concatenating independently precomputed document caches.
    \item \textbf{KVLink~\cite{yang2025kvlink}:} Introduces trainable linking tokens and position-adjustment layers to reconcile independently encoded document caches. While effective, it modifies the model architecture and requires additional training, making it incompatible with off-the-shelf, quantized models distributed on mobile app stores.
    \item \textbf{PerCache~\cite{liu2026percache}:} Explores predictive hierarchical caching for personalized mobile RAG, pre-loading user profiles and historical interactions based on access pattern prediction. PerCache focuses on user personalization and predictive pre-warming; it does not address cryptographic evidence versioning, document revocation, or selective multi-chunk cache repair.
    \item \textbf{Server-Scale Cache Management (vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm}, SGLang):} Server-scale engines employ PagedAttention and RadixAttention trees to share common system prompts across concurrent web requests in GPU high-bandwidth memory (HBM). These systems assume vast in-memory pools and continuous server availability; they do not address cold flash I/O, device thermal envelopes, or volatile memory eviction on Android.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{KV-Cache Quantization and Compression}
To alleviate the memory footprint of KV caches, quantization schemes such as KIVI~\cite{liu2024kivi} compress Key and Value tensors to 2-bit or 4-bit representations. Other approaches such as $\text{H}_2\text{O}$~\cite{zhang2024h2o} evict non-essential tokens based on cumulative attention scores (heavy hitters). While these techniques reduce resident memory size, they do not eliminate prompt prefill latency for new documents and do not provide mechanisms for detecting evidence staleness.

\subsection{Differentiation of \systemname{}}
\systemname{} does not claim that the primitive of saving or loading a KV cache to disk is novel. Rather, the central contribution of \systemname{} lies in:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Evidence-Version-Aware Binding:} Binding every persistent KV segment to a cryptographic SHA-256 hash of its constituent document chunks.
    \item \textbf{Selective Repair Policy:} Detecting document staleness, invalidating affected cache segments, and executing minimal online re-prefill while preserving valid prefix KV state.
    \item \textbf{JNI Context-Budget Safety:} Dynamically validating headroom constraints and boundary-token behavior on low-memory mobile architectures.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Quantification of Flash vs. Compute Trade-offs:} Rigorously measuring the latency and I/O overheads of physical flash restoration versus mobile CPU prefill across distinct hardware tiers.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Questions and Contributions}
\label{sec:rq_contributions}

\subsection{Research Questions}
This investigation is guided by four concrete, falsifiable research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Scaling and Context Independence):} How does physical prompt-prefill compute reduction scale with the number of cached prefix tokens on mobile CPUs across both budget and flagship tiers, and does allocated context capacity ($n_{\text{ctx}}$) exert a statistically significant effect on prefill latency reduction?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Freshness and Staleness Invalidation):} Can a lightweight, cryptographic evidence-versioning policy eliminate the silent stale-KV hallucination vulnerability when underlying documents undergo updates, revocations, and reorderings, without requiring full scratch prefill?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Multi-Hop Factual Integrity):} Does partial KV-cache prefix reuse preserve complete factual accuracy in multi-chunk RAG workloads where answer formulation requires cross-attention synthesis across multiple distinct retrieved passages?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Systems Trade-Off and I/O Overhead):} What is the empirical latency and storage trade-off between reading serialized KV caches from physical mobile flash storage (UFS 2.2 vs. UFS 4.0) and executing native CPU prefill computation?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Primary Contributions}
The primary contributions of this paper are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The \systemname{} Architecture:} We design and implement an evidence-version-aware mobile RAG runtime that couples canonical chunk layout with cryptographic hashing, context-budget safety enforcement, and selective downstream cache repair.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Scaling Benchmark (90 Matched Pairs):} We conduct a matched-workload evaluation across 9 configurations ($K \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ chunks, $n_{\text{ctx}} \in \{512, 1024, 2048\}$) on a commercial Android smartphone. We demonstrate prompt-prefill compute reductions of 19.34--21.21\% for $K=1$, 38.53--40.37\% for $K=2$, and 86.06--86.67\% for $K=4$, delivering up to $3.65\times$ wall speedup.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Device Generalization on Flagship Hardware:} We evaluate \systemname{} on a flagship Samsung Galaxy S25+ (Snapdragon 8 Elite, 12~GB LPDDR5X, UFS 4.0). Despite a $4.04\times$ raw compute speedup over the budget baseline, relative prefill compute reductions remain invariant (16.35\% at $K=1$, 36.73\% at $K=2$, 85.23\% at $K=4$, $\Delta = 1.08\%$).
    \item \textbf{Statistical Analysis of Context Allocation:} Using balanced Two-Way ANOVAs on both platforms, we demonstrate that compute reduction is driven solely by cached token count ($p < 10^{-15}$), with no statistically significant main effect ($p = 0.449$ on Device A; $p = 0.650$ on Device B) or interaction ($p > 0.72$) from context capacity.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Chunk Factual Integrity with Latency Isolation:} Across repeated paired trials on multi-hop QA cases, we isolate native CPU prefill latency from flash load overhead, demonstrating an average prefill compute reduction of 44.13\% and wall speedup of $1.63\times$ while preserving 100\% multi-hop factual recall and confirming that incomplete evidence ablations fail.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Staleness Suite (5 Scenarios):} We evaluate 5 controlled dynamic evidence scenarios, showing that ordinary naive session reuse produces silent stale hallucinations or runtime crashes, whereas \systemname{} reliably detects changes, invalidates affected segments, repairs downstream tokens, or safely abstains.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Explicit Non-Claims and Evidence Boundaries}
To maintain rigorous scientific standards, we delineate what this study does \emph{not} claim:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We do \emph{not} claim that serializing and restoring KV caches is inherently novel; our contribution lies in the evidence-version-aware binding, selective repair policy, context-budget safety, and empirical mobile systems characterization.
    \item We do \emph{not} claim a universal invariance law across all possible transformer architectures or mobile SoCs. All empirical claims are strictly bounded to the evaluated hardware platforms (Qualcomm Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 and Snapdragon 8 Elite), runtime (\texttt{llama.rn} v0.5.8 / \texttt{llama.cpp}), and model (Gemma~2~2B Instruct Q4\_K\_M).
    \item We do \emph{not} claim that the five dynamic test scenarios constitute mathematical proof of universal correctness across arbitrary document modifications. They represent empirical proof-of-concept demonstrations of failure modes that ordinary session reuse cannot survive.
\end{itemize}

The architecture of \systemname{} is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}. It comprises five tightly integrated subsystems: (1) Evidence Ingestion and Version Tracking; (2) Canonical Retrieval and Layout; (3) Adaptive Cache Decision Engine; (4) Context-Budget Safety Guard; and (5) Native Execution and Telemetry Profiler.

\subsection{Evidence Ingestion and Version Tracking}
In an on-device personal knowledge base, text documents (e.g., policy documents, notes, manuals) are partitioned into discrete chunks $\mathcal{C} = \{C_1, C_2, \dots, C_M\}$. Unlike conventional mobile vector databases that store only text strings and embedding vectors, \systemname{} computes and maintains a cryptographic digest for each chunk:
\begin{equation}
    H(C_i) = \text{SHA-256}\bigl(\text{Canonicalize}(C_i)\bigr).
\end{equation}
This cryptographic digest is persisted in an on-device SQLite database alongside the chunk's creation timestamp, unique document ID, and token count. Any modification, deletion, or addition to a document produces an immediate mismatch in $H(C_i)$.

\subsection{Canonical Evidence Layout}
A major challenge in multi-chunk RAG caching is the \emph{chunk permutation problem}. Given $k$ retrieved chunks relevant to a query, standard retrievers return chunks ranked by relevance score. If relevance scores fluctuate slightly across subsequent queries, the retriever may return permutations such as $[C_2, C_1]$ instead of $[C_1, C_2]$.

In causal attention mechanisms, exchanging the positions of $C_1$ and $C_2$ alters the entire token sequence, rendering any previously saved prefix KV cache useless (0\% reuse). \systemname{} addresses this by enforcing a \emph{Canonical Evidence Layout}. When $k$ chunks are selected for prompt construction, \systemname{} groups the chunks and applies a deterministic ordering policy:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{\text{canon}} = \text{Sort}\bigl(\{C_1, \dots, C_k\}\bigr).
\end{equation}
By sorting chunks according to their persistent document and chunk identifiers, the textual prefix sequence is stabilized across queries, maximizing exact prefix cache hits regardless of minor variations in retriever ranking scores.

\subsection{The Adaptive Cache Decision Engine}
When a user issues query $Q$, the system constructs the candidate prompt prefix from the canonically ordered retrieved chunks:
\begin{equation}
    P = \text{FormatPrompt}\bigl(\pi_{\text{canon}}(\{C_1, \dots, C_k\})\bigr).
\end{equation}
The engine computes the composite prefix hash:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K}_{\text{session}} = \text{SHA-256}(P).
\end{equation}
The Decision Engine inspects local flash storage for an active session binary matching $\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}$ and determines the execution strategy:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Case 1 (Exact Prefix Hit):} A valid session file matching $\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}$ exists, and all underlying chunk hashes $H(C_i)$ match the current corpus state. The engine instructs the native runtime to load the KV session from flash. Only the query suffix $Q$ is evaluated online.
    \item \textbf{Case 2 (Selective Downstream Repair):} A partial prefix match exists (e.g., chunks $C_1, \dots, C_{j}$ are unchanged, but chunk $C_{j+1}$ was modified or newly inserted). The engine loads the prefix KV cache up to chunk $C_j$, preserves the valid resident KV tensors, and triggers online prefill exclusively for the modified chunk $C_{j+1}$ and query $Q$.
    \item \textbf{Case 3 (Safe Invalidation and Scratch Fallback):} If a document chunk was revoked or deleted, or if the session binary is corrupted, missing, or fails size/header validation, the engine invalidates the cached session, purges orphaned cache files, and initiates clean scratch prefill from token position 0.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Native Execution and Telemetry Profiler}
The execution layer interfaces with \texttt{llama.rn} (v0.5.8), a React Native JNI wrapper around \texttt{llama.cpp}. \systemname{} intercepts native execution to collect granular systems telemetry:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Kernel I/O Instrumentation:} Polling Linux kernel counters via \texttt{/proc/\{PID\}/io} before and after execution to measure exact physical storage bytes read (\texttt{read\us bytes}), bytes written (\texttt{write\us bytes}), and I/O system calls (\texttt{syscr}, \texttt{syscw}). This enables separating physical flash reads from Linux page-cache hits.
    \item \textbf{Memory Footprint:} Capturing Android Total Proportional Set Size (PSS) via \texttt{dumpsys meminfo} to detect memory expansion and prevent LMK eviction.
    \item \textbf{SoC Telemetry:} Monitoring battery temperature and CPU clock frequencies via Android sysfs to account for thermal throttling across repeated benchmark runs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evidence Versioning, Cache Validation, and Selective Repair}
\label{sec:versioning_repair}

\subsection{Vulnerability of Blind Session Reuse}
In standard mobile implementations of \texttt{llama.cpp} and \texttt{llama.rn}, session caching is exposed via simple file persistence:
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
llama_save_session_file(ctx,
    "session.bin", tokens, n_tokens);
llama_load_session_file(ctx,
    "session.bin", tokens, n_max, &n_out);
\end{verbatim}
}
When used blindly in RAG applications, this interface is dangerous. The runtime assumes that any session stored at \texttt{session.bin} is authoritative. It does not inspect whether the documents referenced during the initial prefill still exist, whether their contents have changed, or whether their ordering has been altered. If a cached session is restored after a document modification, the Transformer attention layers attend directly to stale Key-Value vectors. The model will confidently generate outdated facts, a failure mode undetectable by downstream text decoders.

\subsection{The HACER Selective Repair Policy}
\systemname{} replaces blind persistence with a stateful, hash-verified cache lifecycle (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hacer_repair}).

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{HACER Adaptive Cache Validation and Repair}
\label{alg:hacer_repair}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Retrieved chunks $\mathcal{C} = [C_1, \dots, C_k]$, query $Q$, cache registry $\mathcal{R}$
\ENSURE Validated KV state in the LLM context arena
\STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{canon}} \gets \text{CanonicalSort}(\mathcal{C})$
\STATE $P \gets \text{FormatPrefix}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{canon}})$
\STATE $\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}} \gets \text{SHA-256}(P)$
\IF{$\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}} \in \mathcal{R}$ \AND $\text{VerifyHashes}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{canon}}, \mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}])$}
    \STATE \COMMENT{Case 1: full exact prefix hit}
    \STATE $\text{Status} \gets \text{LoadSessionFromFlash}(\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}].\text{path})$
    \IF{$\text{Status} = \text{SUCCESS}$}
        \STATE $\text{PrefillOnline}(Q)$
        \RETURN
    \ENDIF
\ENDIF
\STATE \COMMENT{Case 2: find longest valid prefix}
\STATE $j^{*} \gets$ largest $j \in [0, k-1]$ such that $\text{SHA-256}(\text{FormatPrefix}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{canon}}[1{:}j])) \in \mathcal{R}$
\IF{$j^{*} > 0$}
    \STATE $\mathcal{K}_{\text{prefix}} \gets \text{SHA-256}(\text{FormatPrefix}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{canon}}[1{:}j^{*}]))$
    \STATE $\text{Status} \gets \text{LoadSessionFromFlash}(\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}_{\text{prefix}}].\text{path})$
    \IF{$\text{Status} = \text{SUCCESS}$}
        \STATE $\text{TokensToEvaluate} \gets \text{Tokenize}(\mathcal{C}_{\text{canon}}[j^{*}{+}1{:}k] \mathbin{\Vert} Q)$
        \STATE $\text{PrefillOnline}(\text{TokensToEvaluate})$
        \STATE $\text{SaveSessionToFlash}(\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}, \mathcal{C}_{\text{canon}})$
        \RETURN
    \ENDIF
\ENDIF
\STATE \COMMENT{Case 3: invalidation, cleanup, and scratch fallback}
\STATE $\text{PurgeInvalidSessions}(\mathcal{R})$
\STATE $\text{ResetContextArena}()$
\STATE $\text{TokensAll} \gets \text{Tokenize}(P \mathbin{\Vert} Q)$
\STATE $\text{PrefillOnline}(\text{TokensAll})$
\STATE $\text{SaveSessionToFlash}(\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}, \mathcal{C}_{\text{canon}})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Context-Budget Safety and Boundary-Aware Prefix Alignment}
\label{sec:context_safety}

\subsection{Dynamic Context Budgeting on Mobile Platforms}
Mobile operating systems enforce aggressive process management through the Low Memory Killer daemon (LMKD). In React Native and native Android execution environments, initializing a large context window ($n_{\text{ctx}} \ge 4096$) can allocate upwards of 1.2~GB of contiguous memory exclusively for KV caches, triggering immediate process termination on 4~GB devices.

To guarantee that on-device RAG operations never crash the host application or trigger ungraceful context truncation, \systemname{} introduces a JNI-level \emph{Context-Budget Safety Guard}. The guard enforces the following headroom constraint prior to initiating inference:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \text{Headroom}(n_{\text{ctx}}, N_{\text{prompt}}, N_{\text{predict}}) ={}& (n_{\text{ctx}} - \Delta_{\text{reserved}}) \\
    & - (N_{\text{prompt}} + N_{\text{predict}}) \ge 0,
\end{split}
\label{eq:headroom}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\text{reserved}} = 4$ represents internal tokens reserved by the \texttt{llama.cpp} core engine for sequence demarcation and special end-of-turn tokens, $N_{\text{prompt}}$ is the total token length of the canonical prompt, and $N_{\text{predict}}$ is the requested generation budget.

If the headroom calculation indicates a budget violation ($\text{Headroom} < 0$), \systemname{} rejects the request at the React Native bridge layer rather than allowing the native engine to silently truncate prompt context. In our experimental evaluations across all nine configurations, the enforced headroom values were:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $n_{\text{ctx}} = 512$: $(512 - 4) - (395 + 35) = 78$~tokens;
    \item $n_{\text{ctx}} = 1024$: $(1024 - 4) - (395 + 35) = 590$~tokens;
    \item $n_{\text{ctx}} = 2048$: $(2048 - 4) - (395 + 35) = 1614$~tokens.
\end{itemize}
Across all 182 recorded benchmark executions, the reported headroom matched the theoretical budget calculation exactly, with zero native engine truncations (\texttt{truncated == 0}).

\subsection{The Continuation Boundary-Token Invariant}
In naive implementations of session restoration, developers assume that if $N_{\text{loaded}}$ tokens are restored from disk for a prompt of total length $N_{\text{prompt}}$, the native prefill engine will evaluate exactly $N_{\text{prompt}} - N_{\text{loaded}}$ tokens online.

However, detailed code-level tracing of \texttt{llama.rn} (v0.5.8) and \texttt{llama.cpp} reveals a subtle but crucial systems behavior: \emph{the boundary token re-evaluation}. When a session is restored, the engine retains KV entries up to index $N_{\text{loaded}} - 1$. When tokenizing the continuation string, Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) merges the leading whitespace or delimiter with preceding context. To guarantee that the attention representation of the first newly introduced token incorporates complete cross-boundary context, the runtime executes prefill starting from index $N_{\text{loaded}} - 1$. Consequently, for $N_{\text{loaded}} > 0$, the number of newly evaluated tokens $N_{\text{eval}}$ adheres strictly to the following invariant:
\begin{equation}
    N_{\text{eval}} = N_{\text{prompt}} - N_{\text{loaded}} + 1.
    \label{eq:boundary_token}
\end{equation}
For baseline runs starting from a cold context ($N_{\text{loaded}} = 0$), $N_{\text{eval}} = N_{\text{prompt}} = 395$.

Table~\ref{tab:token_accounting} confirms this token accounting across all configurations evaluated in our physical benchmark. Failing to account for this $+1$ boundary token in naive implementations leads to off-by-one prefill errors, corrupted positional embeddings, or incorrect compute attribution.

        \item memory footprint (PSS) from Android \texttt{dumpsys meminfo};
        \item the full generated text string for byte-level output validation.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Baselines and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:baselines_metrics}

\subsection{Evaluated System Configurations}
We evaluate three system paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full Prefill (Baseline):} The standard operational mode of mobile LLM deployments. When a query is received, the entire prompt (evidence chunks and query) is tokenized and evaluated from scratch in a freshly initialized context arena ($N_{\text{loaded}} = 0$).
    \item \textbf{Ordinary Blind Session Reuse:} The native session restoration mechanism provided by \texttt{llama.rn}. The system restores a previously serialized session file directly from disk based on a static filename or ID, without verifying whether underlying evidence chunks have been edited, revoked, or permuted.
    \item \textbf{HACER Adaptive Policy (Proposed):} The complete \systemname{} system, incorporating canonical layout sorting, SHA-256 evidence chunk verification, JNI context-budget enforcement, continuation boundary alignment, and selective downstream cache repair.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
To provide a complete systems and NLP characterization, we define the following metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Native Prefill Compute Latency ($T_{\text{prefill}}$, seconds):} The CPU execution time expended during the prompt evaluation phase, recorded directly by the internal timer of \texttt{llama.cpp} (\texttt{timings.prompt\us ms}).
    \item \textbf{Prefill Compute Reduction ($\Delta_{\text{prefill}}$, \%):} The relative reduction in prefill compute latency achieved by KV reuse relative to the matched baseline run:
    \begin{equation}
        \Delta_{\text{prefill}} = \frac{T_{\text{prefill}}^{\text{base}} - T_{\text{prefill}}^{\text{reuse}}}{T_{\text{prefill}}^{\text{base}}} \times 100\%.
    \end{equation}
    To assess estimation uncertainty, we compute 95\% confidence intervals using non-parametric bootstrap resampling with 2,000 iterations.
    \item \textbf{End-to-End Wall-Clock Latency ($T_{\text{wall}}$, seconds):} Total elapsed time from request dispatch at the host bridge to full generation completion, incorporating session I/O, prompt prefill, token decode, and bridge serialization overheads.
    \item \textbf{Wall-Clock Speedup Factor ($S_{\text{wall}}$):} The end-to-end acceleration ratio:
    \begin{equation}
        S_{\text{wall}} = \frac{T_{\text{wall}}^{\text{base}}}{T_{\text{wall}}^{\text{reuse}}}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Flash Cache-Load Overhead ($T_{\text{load}}$, milliseconds):} The latency incurred specifically by deserializing and loading the KV-cache binary from physical flash into the LLM context arena.
    \item \textbf{Storage Read Volume and Access Mode:} Physical flash bytes read, captured via \texttt{/proc/\{PID\}/io}. Following mobile storage benchmarking practice, runs where $\ge 85\%$ of the session binary size is read directly from flash are classified as \emph{Cold Flash Reads}, while runs where $\ge 85\%$ is satisfied via the Linux kernel buffer cache are classified as \emph{Warm Page-Cache Hits}.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Hop Factual Recall (\%):} Binary accuracy assessing whether the generated response accurately reports all required facts across multiple retrieved evidence passages.
    \item \textbf{Stale Fact Hallucination Rate (\%):} The percentage of queries where the model outputs superseded or revoked facts following document updates.
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The Storage vs. Computation Asymmetry on Mobile Platforms}
A central finding of our empirical investigation is the dramatic asymmetry between mobile storage bandwidth and mobile CPU inference throughput across hardware generations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dense CPU Prefill Throughput:} On the Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 SoC, evaluating a 395-token prompt using 4 threads requires approximately 54.2~seconds, corresponding to an effective prompt-prefill throughput of only 7.29~tokens/second. On the flagship Snapdragon 8 Elite, prefill requires 11.9--14.2~seconds (28.4~tokens/s). Even on flagship cores, prefill latency is severely noticeable to interactive users.
    \item \textbf{Physical Flash I/O Bandwidth:} In contrast, reading a 37--40~MB serialized KV-cache binary requires 256.0~ms on mid-range UFS 2.2 storage and only 48.0--56.2~ms on flagship UFS 4.0 storage.
\end{itemize}

This represents an operational asymmetry exceeding $150:1$ on Device A and $204:1\text{ to }229:1$ on Device B in favor of storage restoration over online computation. While server-class deployments with high-end GPUs (e.g., NVIDIA H100) achieve prompt-prefill throughputs exceeding thousands of tokens per second, making disk I/O relatively less attractive, mobile platforms operate in a fundamentally inverted regime. On mobile CPUs, persistent flash storage serves as an ultra-fast, energy-efficient cache for expensive floating-point computations.

\subsection{Mobile Memory Pressure and LMK Resilience}
In commercial mobile operating systems, application lifecycle is governed by the Android Low Memory Killer daemon (LMKD). On budget and mid-range devices equipped with 4~GB of total physical RAM, the Android system, SurfaceFlinger compositor, and background services typically consume 1.8 to 2.2~GB.

During our physical benchmark runs, the \texttt{com.chat} process exhibited a Total Proportional Set Size (PSS) of approximately 2.28~GB (2,280,940~KB) on Device A and 1,986--2,176~MB on Device B while hosting the 1.7~GB Gemma~2~2B Q4\_K\_M weights and the allocated context buffer. Under these razor-thin margins on 4~GB handsets, expanding $n_{\text{ctx}}$ to 4096 tokens would immediately trigger an out-of-memory SIGKILL from the LMK.

By enforcing strict JNI-level context budgeting ($n_{\text{ctx}} - 4 - N_{\text{prompt}} - N_{\text{predict}} \ge 0$), \systemname{} enables mobile applications to operate safely with modest context sizes ($n_{\text{ctx}} \in \{512, 1024\}$) without risking silent truncation or process termination. Furthermore, because \systemname{} persists KV state to flash and releases the context arena between user interactions, it prevents background RAM bloat, allowing the handset to maintain normal multitasking responsiveness.

\subsection{Thermal and Energy Implications}
Sustained CPU execution at peak clock frequencies rapidly elevates device temperature. In continuous evaluation runs on Device A, device battery temperature rose from 29.5$^\circ$C to 38.2$^\circ$C, causing the SoC's dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS) governor to throttle clock rates down to 1.6~GHz.

By eliminating up to 86.67\% of prompt-prefill computation (approximately 46.7 to 47.3~seconds of heavy CPU execution per query on Device A, and 10.8 to 11.5~seconds on Device B), \systemname{} shortens the duration of high-power CPU residency. This dramatic reduction in active processor cycles not only preserves battery life but also prevents thermal throttling, ensuring that subsequent token decoding executes at maximum interactive responsiveness.

\subsection{Architectural Implications for Mobile RAG Systems}
The findings of this paper suggest that on-device RAG systems cannot simply port server-side caching techniques without adapting to mobile realities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Evidence Binding is Mandatory:} In personal workspaces where local documents are continuously synced or edited, blind session caching is an unacceptable liability. Cryptographic chunk hashing provides an indispensable safety layer with microsecond overhead.
    \item \textbf{Canonical Layout Eliminates Permutation Penalty:} Enforcing canonical evidence ordering overcomes the fragility of causal attention masks, ensuring high cache hit rates across varying retriever score distributions.
    \item \textbf{Selective Downstream Repair Maximizes Efficiency:} By preserving valid prefix KV segments and re-prefilling only modified downstream chunks, runtimes can maintain freshness while preserving the bulk of compute savings.
    \item \textbf{Flagship Hardware Still Requires Prefill Optimization:} Our empirical results on Snapdragon 8 Elite disprove the assumption that prefill caching is only relevant for low-end hardware. Accelerating prefill from 13~seconds to 1.8~seconds is the critical dividing line between unacceptable lag and fluid interactive conversational UX.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Threats to Internal Validity}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Operating System and Background Activity:} Commercial Android devices host non-deterministic background daemons (e.g., Google Play Services, carrier telemetry, and memory compactors). As demonstrated in our analysis of the preliminary Case~3 result, an uncoordinated kernel compaction event can temporarily throttle memory bandwidth. We mitigated this threat by enforcing matched-pair execution, randomizing trial order within pairs, incorporating explicit context-flushing intervals, recording Linux kernel I/O counters, and reporting non-parametric bootstrap confidence intervals across repeated trials.
    \item \textbf{Thermal Throttling Dynamics:} Mobile SoCs adjust clock frequencies dynamically based on junction temperatures. To prevent thermal bias, baseline and reuse trials were interleaved, and thermal states were tracked via \texttt{dumpsys battery}.
    \item \textbf{Quarantined Incomplete Trials:} Two incomplete trials out of 182 were quarantined on Device A due to external interruptions (a carrier dialog focus event and a background service timeout). Because both quarantined trials were documented with full telemetry and excluded prior to paired analysis, they introduce no systematic selection bias into the reported results.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Threats to External Validity}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hardware Platform Generalizability:} We evaluated two physical Android smartphones representing mid-range (Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, 4~GB RAM, UFS 2.2) and premium flagship (Snapdragon 8 Elite, 12~GB RAM, UFS 4.0) tiers. While this demonstrates cross-generational generalization across Cortex-A78 and custom Oryon cores, performance characteristics may vary on SoCs featuring dedicated NPU accelerators (e.g., Qualcomm Hexagon, MediaTek APU) or alternative flash storage standards (eMMC 5.1).
    \item \textbf{Model Architecture and Quantization:} We evaluated Gemma~2~2B Instruct quantized to 4-bit integers (\texttt{Q4\_K\_M}). While Gemma~2 employs modern architectural techniques (interleaved local and global attention, Grouped-Query Attention, and Rotary Position Embeddings), models with fundamentally different context mechanisms (such as pure sliding-window attention or state-space models like Mamba) may exhibit distinct prefix caching trade-offs.
    \item \textbf{Scope of Dynamic Evidence Scenarios:} While the five dynamic corpus scenarios rigorously demonstrate that blind session reuse suffers from silent stale hallucinations and crashes, they represent targeted proof-of-concept stress tests rather than an exhaustive survey of all possible document mutation patterns.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Executing Retrieval-Augmented Generation on mobile devices is fundamentally constrained by the severe computational cost of prompt prefill on low-power CPUs. While reusing precomputed Key-Value caches can eliminate redundant prefill computation, existing mobile inference runtimes treat KV-cache files as opaque session snapshots, introducing grave vulnerabilities: silent stale-cache hallucinations, unmanaged context-budget overflows, and continuation boundary misalignments.

In this paper, we presented \systemname{}, an evidence-version-aware inference runtime that makes KV-cache reuse reliable and context-safe on commodity Android smartphones. \systemname{} pairs a canonical evidence layout with cryptographic chunk-level hashing to bind persistent KV tensors to explicit document versions. Under corpus mutations, \systemname{} executes a selective repair policy that preserves valid prefix KV tensors while triggering online prefill only for updated downstream tokens. Furthermore, \systemname{} introduces a JNI-level context-budget safety guard that dynamically enforces headroom constraints and accounts for continuation boundary-token alignment.

We evaluated \systemname{} across two physical Android hardware tiers: a mid-range Xiaomi Redmi 12 5G (Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, UFS 2.2) and a flagship Samsung Galaxy S25+ (Snapdragon 8 Elite, UFS 4.0). The empirical results demonstrate that \systemname{} reduces prompt-prefill compute latency by up to 86.67\% on mid-range hardware and 86.20\% on flagship hardware, saving up to 47.3~seconds of CPU execution and delivering up to $3.65\times$ end-to-end wall speedup with 100\% deterministic output parity and zero engine truncations. Despite a $4.04\times$ raw compute speedup between Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 and Snapdragon 8 Elite, prefill compute reductions at $K=4$ remain invariant within 1.08\%. Balanced Two-Way ANOVAs on both platforms confirmed that compute reductions are governed by cached token count ($p < 10^{-15}$), with no statistically significant effect from allocated context capacity ($p = 0.449\text{--}0.650$). In repeated multi-chunk trials, \systemname{} achieved 100\% multi-hop factual recall across both platforms, while UFS 4.0 flash storage enabled a physical storage-to-compute speedup multiplier of $204\times\text{--}229\times$. Across five controlled dynamic corpus scenarios, \systemname{} eliminated silent stale hallucinations and runtime crashes where naive session reuse failed.

By characterizing the $150{:}1$ to $229{:}1$ operational asymmetry between mobile flash storage bandwidth and CPU prefill throughput across mobile tiers, this work establishes that evidence-version-aware KV-cache reuse provides a practical, robust foundation for high-performance, private, and fresh on-device conversational AI.
