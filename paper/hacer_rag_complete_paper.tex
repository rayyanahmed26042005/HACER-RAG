% Abstract

\begin{abstract}
On-device Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) enables privacy-preserving, zero-cloud question answering directly on consumer smartphones. However, executing autoregressive Small Language Models (SLMs) on mobile processors incurs severe prompt-prefill latency when repeatedly encoding retrieved context. While precomputing and restoring Key-Value (KV) cache prefixes can bypass redundant prefill computation, existing mobile runtimes treat KV-cache files as opaque session snapshots. This introduces a critical systems failure mode: \emph{silent stale-cache hallucination}, where outdated or revoked retrieved evidence preserved in serialized KV caches causes the model to generate factually incorrect responses without detection. Furthermore, dynamic context-capacity constraints and continuation-token boundary effects frequently trigger out-of-memory (OOM) aborts or silent truncation on memory-limited handsets.

In this paper, we present \textbf{HACER-RAG} (\textbf{H}ierarchical \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{C}ache, \textbf{E}vidence-version-aware \textbf{R}epair for on-device RAG), a mobile inference system designed for reliable, fresh, and efficient local RAG across diverse Android hardware tiers. HACER-RAG pairs a canonical evidence layout with cryptographic chunk-level SHA-256 hashing to bind every KV cache segment to an explicit evidence version. Under corpus mutations, HACER-RAG executes a selective repair policy that preserves valid prefix KV tensors while triggering online prefill only for updated downstream chunks, falling back safely to clean scratch prefill under cache corruption. In addition, HACER-RAG introduces a JNI-level context-budget safety guard that dynamically enforces headroom constraints and models continuation boundary-token alignment.

We evaluate HACER-RAG through matched-pair physical experiments on two commercial Android smartphones spanning distinct hardware tiers: a mid-range baseline (\textbf{Xiaomi Redmi 12 5G}, Qualcomm Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, 4~GB LPDDR4X, UFS 2.2 flash) and a flagship platform (\textbf{Samsung Galaxy S25+}, Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite, 12~GB LPDDR5X, UFS 4.0 flash) executing a 4-bit quantized Gemma 2 2B model via \texttt{llama.rn} and \texttt{llama.cpp}. Across a 9-configuration physical scaling matrix ($K \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ chunks, $n_{\text{ctx}} \in \{512, 1024, 2048\}$ tokens), HACER-RAG reveals a \textbf{hardware-invariant scaling law}: despite a $4.04\times$ raw compute speedup on Snapdragon 8 Elite, prompt-prefill compute reductions remain virtually identical between platforms ($86.31\%$ on Device A vs. $85.23\%$ on Device B at $K=4$, a delta of only $1.08\%$). Balanced Two-Way ANOVAs on both platforms confirm that prefill savings are strictly governed by cached token count ($p < 10^{-13}$), with no statistically significant main effect from context capacity ($p = 0.449$ on Device A; $p = 0.650$ on Device B). Furthermore, Device B's UFS 4.0 storage achieves sequential read throughput of $1.7\text{ GB/s}$, loading resident KV caches in $35.6\text{--}56.2\text{ ms}$ while saving $10.8\text{--}11.5\text{ seconds}$ of CPU compute---demonstrating a physical \textbf{Storage-to-Compute Speedup Multiplier of $204\times\text{ to }229\times$}. Across repeated multi-hop RAG queries and five dynamic corpus failure scenarios (content updates, policy revocations, chunk reordering, partial updates, and cache corruption), HACER-RAG delivers 100\% factual recall and completely eliminates stale hallucinations and runtime crashes where ordinary session reuse fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Executing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) locally on mobile devices offers compelling advantages for personal privacy, offline autonomy, and predictable service availability~\cite{lewis2020retrieval,wu2024mobilellm}. In an on-device RAG workflow, a mobile retriever extracts relevant evidence passages from a local knowledge base (e.g., personal notes, enterprise documents, or cached emails), constructs an augmented prompt, and feeds it into an on-device Small Language Model (SLM) for autoregressive generation~\cite{guu2020realm,liu2026percache}.

However, modern mobile devices face severe hardware and thermal bottlenecks. Handsets in the entry-level to mid-range tier typically operate on budget SoCs (such as the Qualcomm Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, featuring 2$\times$ Cortex-A78 performance cores and 6$\times$ Cortex-A55 efficiency cores) paired with 4--6~GB of shared LPDDR4X memory~\cite{reddi2021mlperfmobile}. Even modern flagship devices (such as the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite featuring high-frequency custom Oryon cores) remain thermally throttled under continuous execution. When executing 2-billion to 4-billion parameter language models quantized to 4-bit integers (e.g., Gemma 2 2B Instruct Q4\_K\_M~\cite{gemma2024gemma2}), inference latency is heavily bifurcated:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Autoregressive Decode Phase:} Generating output tokens proceeds sequentially at 20--50 tokens per second, bound primarily by memory bandwidth and per-token vector-matrix products.
    \item \textbf{Prompt Prefill Phase:} Evaluating the prompt (system instructions, multiple retrieved document chunks, conversation history, and user query) requires dense matrix-matrix evaluations across all transformer layers. On a mid-range phone CPU, prefilling a 395-token prompt takes over $54$~seconds. Even on a flagship Snapdragon 8 Elite, prefilling the same prompt requires $12.8\text{--}14.2$~seconds of sustained CPU execution, severely degrading conversational responsiveness.
\end{enumerate}

When users ask consecutive questions against the same local document corpus or personal workspace, the retriever repeatedly selects identical or partially overlapping document chunks~\cite{lu2024turborag,liu2026percache}. In traditional inference runtimes, the mobile processor is forced to re-encode these static chunks from scratch on every interaction, dissipating scarce battery capacity and inducing thermal throttling.

While caching precomputed Key-Value (KV) tensors appears as a natural optimization, saving and restoring KV caches on mobile platforms introduces major system challenges that existing LLM engines do not solve. In particular, mobile runtimes such as \texttt{llama.rn} and \texttt{llama.cpp}~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp} treat KV-cache files as opaque session snapshots designed for conversational turn resumption. Applying these session primitives blindly to on-device RAG exposes three critical vulnerabilities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Silent Stale-KV Hallucination:} If a local document is edited, updated, or revoked (e.g., a corporate travel policy, personal medical record, or pricing schedule), naive session restoration reloads outdated KV tensors into the model's resident attention memory. Because the native engine cannot distinguish fresh evidence from stale cache bytes, the model generates hallucinated, obsolete facts with high confidence, even though the on-disk document index has been updated.
    \item \textbf{Context Headroom Violations:} Allocating static context buffers on low-RAM handsets risks triggering silent engine truncations or Out-Of-Memory (OOM) process termination by the Android Low Memory Killer (LMK). Mobile RAG runtimes lack dynamic context budgeting that reconciles native engine limits with variable prompt and generation budgets.
    \item \textbf{Continuation Boundary Misalignment:} Concatenating variable query text to a precomputed KV cache prefix without accounting for tokenizer boundary-merging behaviors causes native runtime index misalignments, producing garbled generation or redundant token re-evaluation.
\end{itemize}

To address these challenges, we present \textbf{HACER-RAG} (\textbf{H}ierarchical \textbf{A}daptive \textbf{C}ache, \textbf{E}vidence-version-aware \textbf{R}epair for on-device RAG). HACER-RAG is an on-device RAG runtime architecture that transforms raw KV-cache serialization into an evidence-version-aware, context-safe, and selectively repairable caching system for commodity mobile devices.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
This paper makes four primary contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Architecture for Evidence-Version-Aware KV Caching:} We design HACER-RAG, coupling canonical chunk layout with cryptographic SHA-256 evidence fingerprinting. This enables deterministic identification of cached evidence segments and guarantees that stale KV tensors are never erroneously evaluated.
    \item \textbf{Selective Downstream Repair \& Safe Fallback:} We implement an online selective repair algorithm that preserves valid prefix KV tensors up to the first point of divergence and re-prefills only modified downstream chunks, backed by a crash-resilient fallback mechanism.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Tier Physical Hardware Validation:} We conduct an extensive physical evaluation spanning two commercial Android handsets representing distinct architectural tiers: the mid-range Xiaomi Redmi 12 5G (Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, UFS 2.2) and the flagship Samsung Galaxy S25+ (Snapdragon 8 Elite, UFS 4.0), logging over 225 matched physical trials.
    \item \textbf{Hardware-Invariant Scaling \& Flash Asymmetry Proof:} We empirically prove that prompt-prefill compute reduction follows a hardware-invariant scaling law ($86.31\%$ on budget vs. $85.23\%$ on flagship at $K=4$), confirmed by Two-Way ANOVAs showing context-capacity invariance ($p > 0.44$ on both SoCs). We further demonstrate a physical \textbf{Storage-to-Compute Speedup Multiplier exceeding $200\times$} enabled by UFS 4.0 flash throughput.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Motivation and Systems Asymmetry}
\label{sec:motivation}

\subsection{The Storage vs. Compute Asymmetry on Mobile Platforms}
On desktop and server-class deployments, high-performance accelerators (such as NVIDIA H100 GPUs) evaluate thousands of prompt tokens per second, making disk I/O a potential bottleneck. On battery-powered mobile devices, however, the compute-to-I/O relationship is fundamentally inverted.

On the Qualcomm Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 SoC, evaluating a 395-token prompt using 4 CPU threads requires an average of $54.2$~seconds (effective prefill throughput of $7.29$~tokens/second). In contrast, reading a 37.5~MB serialized KV cache from the device's internal UFS 2.2 flash storage requires less than $250$~milliseconds. On the flagship Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite, the raw prefill compute latency is $12.8\text{--}14.2$~seconds, while reading the same KV cache from high-speed UFS 4.0 flash storage takes only $48.0\text{--}56.2$~milliseconds.

This establishes an operational asymmetry where mobile CPU floating-point computation is \textbf{$120\times\text{ to }400\times$ slower} than mobile flash storage restoration. Persistent flash storage on smartphones functions as an ultra-fast, energy-efficient cache for expensive transformer attention states.

\subsection{The Stale-KV Hallucination Vulnerability}
Existing mobile LLM wrappers (such as \texttt{llama.rn}) expose opaque session persistence functions (\texttt{saveSession} and \texttt{loadSession}). These APIs operate blindly: they bind a cache file to an arbitrary path or conversational turn number, without any semantic verification of document freshness. 

If an enterprise document or user note is edited, naive session restoration reloads outdated Key-Value representations into the model's resident attention memory. As a result, the attention mechanism operates over obsolete representations, causing the model to output superseded facts with high confidence---a vulnerability we term \emph{Silent Stale-KV Hallucination}.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig1_system_architecture.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{HACER-RAG System Architecture and Inference Pipeline.} Illustrating the four core subsystems: (1) Canonical Evidence Layout Engine, (2) Evidence-Version Matcher with SHA-256 chunk hashing, (3) Selective KV-Cache Repair and Flash Deserializer, and (4) JNI Context-Budget Safety Guard.}
\label{fig:system_architecture}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:system_architecture} illustrates the end-to-end architecture of \systemname. The system operates across four integrated stages:

\subsection{Evidence Chunk Fingerprinting}
When the mobile retriever extracts evidence passages from the local store, each chunk $C_i$ is mapped to a cryptographic evidence atom:
\begin{equation}
    H(C_i) = \text{SHA-256}(\text{Canonicalize}(\text{Text}(C_i)))
\end{equation}
By hashing the canonical text rather than volatile database IDs, HACER ensures that chunk fingerprints are invariant to retrieval ranking shifts and database migrations.

\subsection{Canonical Evidence Ordering}
Autoregressive language models employ causal attention masks, meaning that the KV representation of token $t$ depends on all preceding tokens $1, \dots, t-1$. In conventional RAG pipelines, small fluctuations in retrieval scores often reorder retrieved chunks (e.g., returning $[C_2, C_1]$ instead of $[C_1, C_2]$), completely destroying sequential prefix matching.

HACER-RAG introduces a \textbf{Canonical Evidence Layout Engine} that sorts retrieved evidence chunks according to a deterministic order (e.g., lexicographical chunk URI or hierarchical document structure) before prompt assembly:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_{\text{canonical}} = \text{Sort}(\{C_1, C_2, \dots, C_K\})
\end{equation}
This guarantees that whenever an identical set of chunks is retrieved, the token prefix presented to the inference engine is identical, maximizing prefix cache hits.

\subsection{Evidence-Version Matching and Selective Repair}
Let $\mathcal{C}_{\text{cached}} = [C_1, C_2, \dots, C_M]$ be the ordered chunks represented in a serialized KV cache, and let $\mathcal{C}_{\text{query}} = [C'_1, C'_2, \dots, C'_N]$ be the newly retrieved evidence set. The HACER Matcher identifies the longest common prefix of valid chunks:
\begin{equation}
    k^* = \max \left\{ k \le \min(M, N) \;\middle|\; \forall i \le k, \; H(C_i) = H(C'_i) \right\}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item If $k^* = N$, the entire evidence prefix is valid. HACER loads the full resident KV cache from flash storage and performs online prefill solely for the new query tokens.
    \item If $0 < k^* < N$, HACER executes \textbf{Selective Downstream Repair}: it restores the valid prefix KV tensors up to chunk $k^*$ and initiates online prefill starting at token position $P(k^* + 1)$, evaluating only modified or newly introduced chunks.
    \item If $k^* = 0$, the prefix has diverged at the root. HACER purges the obsolete cache and executes a clean scratch prefill.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Context-Budget Safety Guard}
To eliminate engine truncations and Android Low Memory Killer (LMK) aborts, HACER enforces a strict runtime headroom invariant prior to JNI dispatch:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Headroom} = n_{\text{ctx}} - n_{\text{boundary}} - N_{\text{prompt}} - N_{\text{predict}} \ge 0
\end{equation}
where $n_{\text{ctx}}$ is the allocated context buffer, $n_{\text{boundary}} = 4$ accounts for continuation and BOS/EOS tokens, $N_{\text{prompt}}$ is the native token count, and $N_{\text{predict}}$ is the maximum generation budget. If $\text{Headroom} < 0$, HACER executes an intelligent chunk truncation policy before native execution, guaranteeing zero OOM aborts.

\section{Storage I/O Asymmetry Across Flash Tiers}
\label{sec:storage_io}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig3_io_and_storage_breakdown.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Mobile Storage I/O and Compute Trade-Off Analysis.} (a) Physical flash cache-load overhead vs. CPU prefill compute time saved across UFS 2.2 (Device A) and UFS 4.0 (Device B). (b) Storage-to-compute speedup multiplier across $K \in \{1, 2, 4\}$.}
\label{fig:storage_breakdown}
\end{figure*}

A core premise of HACER-RAG is that loading precomputed KV states from flash storage is drastically faster than recomputing attention states on mobile CPUs. Our dual-device evaluation reveals how this dynamic scales from UFS 2.2 to UFS 4.0:

\subsection{UFS 2.2 vs. UFS 4.0 Throughput (Device A vs. Device B)}
On Device A's UFS 2.2 flash interface, sequential read bandwidth reaches $\sim 280\text{ MB/s}$, requiring $82\text{--}115\text{ ms}$ to load session binaries and delivering a compute-to-I/O speedup multiplier of $127\times\text{ to }411\times$.

On Device B's UFS 4.0 flash interface, measured sequential read throughput reaches \textbf{$1,700\text{ MB/s}$ ($1.7\text{ GB/s}$)}---a \textbf{$6.07\times$ bandwidth increase}. Consequently, loading the 37.5~MB session binary ($K=4$) takes only \textbf{$48.0\text{ to }56.2\text{ ms}$}, saving over $11,000\text{ ms}$ of intense CPU prefill. This yields a physical \textbf{Storage-to-Compute Speedup Multiplier of $204\times\text{ to }229\times$}.

\subsection{Memory Footprint and Stability}
Peak Proportional Set Size (PSS) remained strictly bounded throughout all trials:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Device A: $1,980\text{--}2,140\text{ MB}$ (within 4~GB physical RAM budget).
    \item Device B: $1,986\text{--}2,176\text{ MB}$ (abundant headroom within 12~GB physical RAM budget).
\end{itemize}
Session deserialization produced zero memory leaks or address space fragmentation across consecutive trials.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why Flagship Devices Still Require Prefill Caching}
A potential critique of on-device KV caching is that high-end mobile SoCs with custom cores (such as Snapdragon 8 Elite) might render prefill optimization redundant. Our empirical data refutes this hypothesis:
\begin{itemize}
    \item On Device B, baseline prefill of a 395-token prompt requires \textbf{$12.8\text{--}14.2$~seconds}. For conversational assistants, an initial latency of 14 seconds violates acceptable interactive thresholds.
    \item HACER-RAG reduces prefill latency to \textbf{$1.79\text{--}1.94$~seconds}, delivering a sub-2-second Time-To-First-Token (TTFT).
\end{itemize}
Prefill caching is not a budget hardware workaround; it is a fundamental architectural prerequisite for conversational responsiveness across all mobile tiers.

\subsection{Memory Pressure and LMK Coexistence}
On 4~GB devices, the Android Low Memory Killer daemon (LMKD) poses a constant termination risk. By enforcing the JNI headroom invariant and persisting caches to flash between turns, HACER keeps RAM residency strictly bounded ($\sim 2.1\text{ GB}$ PSS), enabling reliable multi-turn execution without background app eviction.

% 11. Limitations and Threats to Validity
% ==============================================================================
\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{Threats to Internal Validity}
Non-deterministic OS background activity (e.g., memory compaction) can induce transient memory bus contention. We mitigated this by enforcing paired executions, counterbalancing trial order, tracking Linux kernel I/O counters, and computing non-parametric bootstrap confidence intervals across repeated trials.

\subsection{Threats to External Validity}
While our evaluation spans two distinct hardware tiers (ARM Cortex-A78/A55 with UFS 2.2 vs. custom Oryon with UFS 4.0), specialized neural accelerators (e.g., discrete mobile NPUs) were not evaluated. Future work will investigate compiling HACER's chunk-level attention states to mobile NPU graph runtimes (e.g., Qualcomm QNN / MediaTek NeuroPilot).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Executing Retrieval-Augmented Generation on mobile devices is bottlenecked by the heavy computational burden of prompt prefill. While KV-cache reuse can eliminate redundant computation, existing mobile runtimes treat KV caches as opaque session snapshots, introducing grave vulnerabilities: silent stale-cache hallucinations, unmanaged context-budget overflows, and continuation boundary misalignments.

In this paper, we presented \textbf{HACER-RAG}, an evidence-version-aware inference runtime that makes on-device KV caching reliable, fresh, and context-safe. By coupling canonical evidence ordering with cryptographic chunk hashing, HACER-RAG guarantees cache freshness and enables selective repair under document mutations.

Through extensive physical evaluations across both mid-range (Xiaomi Redmi 12 5G) and flagship (Samsung Galaxy S25+) Android smartphones, we proved that HACER-RAG reduces prompt prefill latency by up to \textbf{86.31\%} on budget hardware and \textbf{85.23\%} on flagship hardware, delivering up to \textbf{3.65$\times$} end-to-end wall-clock speedup and dropping prefill latency to sub-2-second thresholds ($1.79\text{ s}$) on Snapdragon 8 Elite. Balanced Two-Way ANOVAs confirmed that context capacity exerts no statistically significant effect on compute reduction across both architectures ($p > 0.44$). Furthermore, with UFS 4.0 flash storage sustaining $1.7\text{ GB/s}$ read throughput, HACER achieves a physical storage-to-compute speedup multiplier of over \textbf{200:1}. By resolving the trade-off between latency and factual freshness, HACER-RAG provides a robust foundation for high-performance, private, on-device conversational AI.
