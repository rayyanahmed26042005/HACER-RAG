\begin{abstract}
On-device Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) enables private, offline question answering, but small language models (SLMs) on mobile CPUs incur high prompt-prefill latency when re-encoding retrieved evidence. Restoring Key-Value (KV) caches avoids redundant computation, yet mobile runtimes treat session files as opaque snapshots. When evidence changes, blind reuse causes silent stale-cache hallucinations, context overflows, and continuation boundary errors. We present \systemname{} (\emph{Hierarchical Adaptive Cache, Evidence-version-aware Repair for on-device RAG}), which binds KV segments to hierarchical SHA-256 chunk and prefix digests under a canonical layout, re-prefills only modified downstream chunks via prefix-preserving selective repair, falls back to scratch prefill on corruption, and enforces a Java Native Interface (JNI) context-budget guard. We evaluate 4-bit Gemma~2~2B via \texttt{llama.rn}/\allowbreak\texttt{llama.cpp} across two physical Android tiers: mid-range (Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, UFS 2.2) and flagship (Snapdragon 8 Elite, UFS 4.0). Across 90 matched pairs and nine configurations ($K\in\{1,2,4\}$ chunks, context capacity $n_{\text{ctx}}\in\{512,1024,2048\}$), the mid-range device reduces prefill compute by 19.34--21.21\%, 38.53--40.37\%, and 86.06--86.67\%, saving up to 47.33~s ($3.65\times$ wall speedup) with zero truncation and 100\% deterministic output parity. The flagship exhibits similar scaling (16.35\%, 36.73\%, 85.23\%; a 1.08 percentage-point gap at $K{=}4$) alongside a $4.04\times$ raw compute speedup. Balanced two-way ANOVAs confirm that cached tokens govern reductions ($p<10^{-15}$), whereas context capacity ($p=0.650\text{--}0.981$) does not. UFS~4.0 restores a 4-chunk session in 48.0--56.2~ms, saving 10.8--11.5~s ($204\times\text{--}229\times$). Multi-hop recall remains 100\%, and \systemname{} eliminates stale hallucinations and crashes across five dynamic-corpus scenarios.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
On-device RAG provides privacy, offline capability, and predictable availability~\cite{lewis2020retrieval,wu2024mobilellm}. A local retriever selects evidence chunks, builds an augmented prompt, and supplies it to an on-device SLM~\cite{guu2020realm,liu2026percache}. Mobile hardware is tightly constrained~\cite{alizadeh2024llmflash,xue2024edgmoe,reddi2021mlperfmobile}: our mid-range testbed pairs a Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 ($2\times$A78 + $6\times$A55) with 4~GB LPDDR4X, while the flagship pairs a Snapdragon 8 Elite with 12~GB LPDDR5X under strict thermal limits. For 4-bit models~\cite{lin2024awq,frantar2022gptq} such as Gemma~2~2B Q4\_K\_M~\cite{gemma2024gemma2}, our measured decode runs at 28--30~tok/s on mid-range cores and 70--80~tok/s on flagship cores, whereas prefilling a 395-token prompt takes 49--56~s and 12--14~s, respectively.

\looseness=-1
Repeated queries against a local corpus frequently return identical or overlapping chunks~\cite{lu2024turborag,liu2026percache}. Conventional runtimes re-encode this evidence per query, draining battery and inducing thermal throttling. KV tensor caching avoids repeated prefill: on Device~A, prefilling 395 tokens takes 54.2~s (7.3~tok/s), while reading a 37--40~MB 4-chunk KV cache from UFS~2.2 takes under 350~ms (and 256.0~ms on average for 2-chunk cold reads). On Device~B (UFS~4.0), prefill takes 13.1~s versus 48.0~ms to load. Prefill is thus $155\times\text{--}212\times$ slower than flash restoration on Device~A and $204\times\text{--}229\times$ on Device~B.

Runtimes such as \texttt{llama.rn} and \texttt{llama.cpp}~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp} expose \texttt{saveSession}/\allowbreak\texttt{loadSession} as opaque file snapshots without semantic validity checks. In on-device RAG, blind persistence causes three failures:
(1)~\emph{Silent stale-KV hallucination:} When documents are edited, redacted, or revoked, naive KV reuse conditions causal attention on outdated prefixes, producing superseded facts~\cite{ji2023hallucination}.
(2)~\emph{Context headroom violations:} Mobile engines allocate a fixed contiguous KV arena ($2LHD\,n_{\text{ctx}}$ elements). Exceeding this capacity either silently drops leading tokens---corrupting positional embeddings---or risks out-of-memory termination by the Android Low Memory Killer Daemon (LMKD).
(3)~\emph{Continuation boundary misalignment:} Appending queries to restored prefixes without accounting for byte-pair encoding (BPE) boundary merges alters token indexing across the prefix--query seam.

\textbf{Research questions.} \textbf{RQ1} (scaling and context independence): How does prefill reduction scale with cached prefix tokens across device tiers, and does allocated context capacity ($n_{\text{ctx}}$) alter savings? \textbf{RQ2} (freshness): Can cryptographic versioning eliminate stale-KV hallucinations under updates, revocations, and reorderings without full scratch prefill? \textbf{RQ3} (multi-hop integrity): Does partial prefix reuse preserve accuracy when answers require joint reasoning across passages? \textbf{RQ4} (I/O trade-off): What is the latency trade-off between mobile flash restoration (UFS~2.2/4.0) and native CPU prefill?

\textbf{Contributions.} (1)~The \systemname{} architecture: hierarchical chunk- and prefix-level SHA-256 hashing, canonical layout, JNI context-budget safety, and prefix-preserving selective repair. (2)~A 90-pair scaling benchmark over nine configurations on Device~A: 19.34\%--86.67\% prefill reduction, saving up to 47.33~s ($3.65\times$ wall speedup). (3)~Cross-tier generalization to a flagship phone: $4.04\times$ raw compute speedup with similar relative scaling (within 1.08 percentage points at $K{=}4$). (4)~Two-way ANOVAs on both tiers proving that cached prefix tokens, not context capacity, govern savings ($p<10^{-15}$ vs.\ $p=0.650\text{--}0.981$). (5)~Multi-chunk validation across three two-hop cases: 44.13\% mean prefill reduction, $1.63\times$ wall speedup, 100\% recall, and 100\%-failing single-chunk ablations. (6)~A five-scenario staleness suite showing that \systemname{} eliminates stale hallucinations and crashes where blind reuse fails in 100\% of mutation tests.

\textbf{Scope and non-claims.} We do not claim that KV serialization itself is novel; our contribution lies in cryptographic evidence binding, prefix-preserving selective repair, JNI budget safety, and empirical mobile characterization. Findings are bounded to the two evaluated phones, \texttt{llama.rn}/\allowbreak\texttt{llama.cpp}, and Gemma~2~2B.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
\textbf{KV-cache fundamentals.} In a decoder-only transformer, token $t$ attends only to positions $\tau\le t$ via a causal mask. Key/Value tensors for a prefix of length $P$ are therefore invariant to subsequent tokens and can be reused directly if weights and Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)~\cite{su2024roformer} remain identical. \texttt{llama.cpp}~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp} serializes the KV arena alongside evaluated token IDs, allowing continuation prefill from index $P$.

\textbf{KV reuse and repair in RAG.} \emph{TurboRAG}~\cite{lu2024turborag} precomputes offline chunk caches using positional re-indexing and fine-tuning. \emph{KVLink}~\cite{yang2025kvlink} inserts trainable linking tokens to stitch independent caches, requiring fine-tuning rather than drop-in deployment with frozen quantized GGUF binaries. \emph{PerCache}~\cite{liu2026percache} predictively caches query-answer pairs and KV segments on mobile devices, but does not enforce cryptographic chunk versioning, revocation, or canonical reordering. Server engines such as vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm} and SGLang~\cite{zheng2024sglang} share prefixes in GPU DRAM without addressing mobile flash I/O or Android memory limits. For non-prefix reuse, \emph{CacheBlend}~\cite{yao2024cacheblend} and recent selective-repair methods (\emph{RelaxKV}, \emph{CacheRepair}, and \emph{CacheClip}~\cite{selective2026preprints}) selectively recompute cross-attention layers on high-memory GPUs. By contrast, \systemname{}'s selective repair operates via deterministic canonical prefix caching with chunk-level invalidation---preserving the longest valid prefix on flash and re-prefilling only from the first mutated chunk onward, requiring zero model modification. Compression schemes such as KIVI~\cite{liu2024kivi} and H$_2$O~\cite{zhang2024h2o} shrink in-memory footprints but neither skip prefill for unencoded text nor detect document staleness.

\textbf{Differentiation.} \systemname{} provides: (i)~hierarchical binding of KV files to individual chunk hashes and composite prefix digests, (ii)~prefix-preserving selective repair upon partial updates, (iii)~JNI context-budget and BPE boundary alignment, and (iv)~UFS~2.2 vs.\ 4.0 flash-to-compute characterization.

\systemname{} comprises five subsystems (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). The term \emph{Hierarchical} reflects its two-level validation metadata: Level~1 tracks individual chunk content digests $H(C_i)$ in SQLite, while Level~2 indexes cumulative prefix session keys $\mathcal{K}_{1:j}=\text{SHA-256}(\text{FormatPrefix}(\mathcal{C}_c[1{:}j]))$ for $j\in\{1,\dots,k\}$, enabling prefix fallback when downstream chunks change.

\textbf{Evidence ingestion and versioning.} Documents are segmented into chunks $\mathcal{C}=\{C_1,\dots,C_M\}$. SQLite stores each chunk with $H(C_i)=\text{SHA-256}(\text{Canonicalize}(C_i))$---where $\text{Canonicalize}(\cdot)$ applies Unicode NFC normalization, converts CRLF to LF, and strips trailing whitespace---alongside timestamp, document ID, and token count. Any mutation alters $H(C_i)$.

\textbf{Canonical evidence layout.} Retriever score fluctuations can permute top-$k$ chunks ($[C_2,C_1]$ vs.\ $[C_1,C_2]$). Under causal attention, a permutation alters every token position and invalidates saved prefixes (0\% reuse). \systemname{} enforces a deterministic order $\pi_{\text{canon}}=\text{Sort}(\{C_1,\dots,C_k\})$ by persistent document and chunk IDs, stabilizing prefixes against ranking noise.

\textbf{Adaptive cache decision engine.} Given canonical chunks $\mathcal{C}_c$, the engine formats prefix $P=\text{FormatPrefix}(\mathcal{C}_c)$, computes $\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}=\text{SHA-256}(P)$, and queries the two-level registry (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hacer_repair}):
\emph{Case~1 (exact hit):} $\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}$ exists and all $H(C_i)$ match; only query $Q$ is prefilled.
\emph{Case~2 (selective repair via prefix invalidation):} When chunk $C_{j^*+1}$ is modified, the engine identifies the longest valid prefix $j^*\in[1,k{-}1]$ whose composite digest $\mathcal{K}_{1:j^*}$ and constituent hashes $H(C_1)..H(C_{j^*})$ match. It loads prefix session $j^*$ and online-prefills only the invalidated tail $C_{j^*+1}..C_k$ plus $Q$.
\emph{Case~3 (safe invalidation):} Missing, revoked, or header-corrupted files trigger cache eviction, arena reset, and scratch prefill.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{HACER hierarchical cache validation and repair}
\label{alg:hacer_repair}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE chunks $\mathcal{C}=[C_1..C_k]$, query $Q$, registry $\mathcal{R}$
\STATE $\mathcal{C}_c\gets\text{CanonicalSort}(\mathcal{C})$; $P\gets\text{FormatPrefix}(\mathcal{C}_c)$; $\mathcal{K}\gets\text{SHA-256}(P)$
\IF{$\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{R}$ \AND VerifyHashes$(\mathcal{C}_c[1{:}k],\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}])$ \AND LoadSession$(\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}])$}
  \STATE \COMMENT{Case 1} PrefillOnline$(Q)$; \textbf{return}
\ENDIF
\STATE $j^{*}\gets\max\{j\in[1,k{-}1] \mid \mathcal{K}_{1:j}\in\mathcal{R} \land \text{VerifyHashes}(\mathcal{C}_c[1{:}j],\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}_{1:j}])\}$
\IF{$j^{*}>0$ \AND LoadSession$(\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}_{1:j^*}])$}
  \STATE \COMMENT{Case 2} PrefillOnline(Tokenize$(\mathcal{C}_c[j^{*}{+}1{:}k]\Vert Q)$); SaveSession$(\mathcal{K})$; \textbf{return}
\ENDIF
\STATE \COMMENT{Case 3} PurgeInvalidSessions; ResetContextArena; PrefillOnline(Tokenize$(P\Vert Q)$); SaveSession$(\mathcal{K})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Native execution and telemetry.} Wrapping \texttt{llama.rn} v0.5.8, the execution layer logs kernel I/O counters from \texttt{/proc/\allowbreak\{PID\}/\allowbreak io} (separating physical flash reads from page-cache hits), Proportional Set Size (PSS) from \texttt{dumpsys meminfo}, and battery temperature and CPU clocks from \texttt{sysfs}.

\textbf{Blind reuse versus HACER.} Standard \texttt{llama\us load\us session\us file} loads \texttt{session.bin} without checking document hashes, yielding 0\% reuse on reordered chunks, stale answers on edits/revocations, and crashes on truncated binaries. \systemname{} guarantees freshness, order invariance, and pre-JNI integrity checks.

\section{Context-Budget Safety and Boundary Alignment}
\label{sec:context_safety}
\textbf{Headroom guard.} Because large KV arenas increase memory pressure on 4~GB devices, we bound active allocations to $n_{\text{ctx}}\le2048$ and enforce a pre-inference JNI guard:
\begin{equation}
(n_{\text{ctx}}-\Delta_{\text{res}})-(N_{\text{prompt}}+N_{\text{predict}})\ge 0,
\label{eq:headroom}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\text{res}}=4$ tokens are reserved by \texttt{llama.cpp} for sequence control markers. Requests violating Eq.~\eqref{eq:headroom} are rejected cleanly before native execution. For $N_{\text{prompt}}=395$ and $N_{\text{predict}}=35$, analytical headroom is 78, 590, and 1614 tokens at $n_{\text{ctx}}=512$, 1024, and 2048; empirically, all 182 scaling runs recorded \texttt{truncated==0}.

\textbf{Boundary-token invariant.} When restoring $N_{\text{loaded}}$ prefix tokens, BPE tokenization can merge leading query whitespace or delimiters with the final prefix token. Consequently, \texttt{llama.rn}/\allowbreak\texttt{llama.cpp} re-evaluates the last loaded token at index $N_{\text{loaded}}-1$ to preserve exact cross-boundary attention. For $N_{\text{loaded}}>0$, the evaluated token count is:
\begin{equation}
N_{\text{eval}}=N_{\text{prompt}}-N_{\text{loaded}}+1.
\label{eq:boundary_token}
\end{equation}
For cold prefill ($N_{\text{loaded}}=0$), $N_{\text{eval}}=395$. For $K\in\{1,2,4\}$ with $N_{\text{loaded}}\in\{84,158,341\}$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:boundary_token} predicts $N_{\text{eval}}\in\{312,238,55\}$, matching native telemetry across all runs.

\textbf{Software and model.} Our React Native host (\texttt{com.chat}) invokes \texttt{llama.rn} v0.5.8 (\texttt{llama.cpp} compiled with ARM NEON and OpenMP) running Gemma~2~2B Instruct \texttt{Q4\_K\_M} ($\sim$1.708~GB; SHA-256 \texttt{e0aee85060f168f0\ldots}). Greedy decoding (temperature~0, $N_{\text{predict}}=35$) ensures deterministic outputs.

\textbf{Workload.} Using a synthetic enterprise corpus (\emph{Zephyr Dynamics}), the scaling prompt combines four chunks and a four-hop question:
Chunk~1: Overview (founded 2011, 847 employees);
Chunk~2: Leadership (CEO Priya Nandakumar, CLO Jorge Fonseca, CTO Marcus L{\"o}bell, CFO Saoirse Hennessey);
Chunk~3: AeroSense X7 (12,000~m ceiling, 72~h battery);
Chunk~4: SwarmKit Pro (2,048 drones, \$1,200 standard / \$3,800 full-capacity tiers).
The query requests the founding year, CLO, battery life, and full-capacity price ($N_{\text{prompt}}=395$ tokens, SHA-256 \texttt{a5f1db09b904e813\ldots27c6d}).

\textbf{Protocol and metrics.}
(i)~Before each baseline run, the KV arena is flushed with an orthogonal prompt so $N_{\text{loaded}}=0$.
(ii)~Within each matched pair, execution order (baseline-first vs.\ reuse-first) is randomized to prevent thermal or background drift bias.
(iii)~We log native prefill time $T_{\text{prefill}}$ (\texttt{prompt\us ms}), decode time (\texttt{predicted\us ms}), bridge wall time $T_{\text{wall}}$, session load time $T_{\text{load}}$ (ms), \texttt{/proc/\allowbreak\{PID\}/\allowbreak io} read bytes, and PSS.
We compute per-pair prefill reduction and wall speedup:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{prefill}}=\frac{T^{\text{base}}_{\text{prefill}}-T^{\text{reuse}}_{\text{prefill}}}{T^{\text{base}}_{\text{prefill}}}\times100\%,\quad
S_{\text{wall}}=\frac{T^{\text{base}}_{\text{wall}}}{T^{\text{reuse}}_{\text{wall}}},
\end{equation}
reporting 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals (1,000 resamples over matched pairs). Using \texttt{/proc/\allowbreak\{PID\}/\allowbreak io} physical read ratios ($\text{\texttt{read\_bytes}}/\text{\texttt{rchar}}$), runs are classified as cold flash reads ($>$85\% fetched from physical UFS storage following cache eviction) or warm page-cache hits ($<$15\% physical I/O). Factual recall (\%)~\cite{min2023factscore} is scored via deterministic exact entity-substring matching for each required hop, and stale-fact hallucination rate (\%) measures the fraction of mutation trials outputting superseded or revoked values.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{Storage--compute asymmetry.} Dense CPU prefill runs at 7.3~tok/s on Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 (54.2~s for 395 tokens) and 28--33~tok/s on Snapdragon 8 Elite (11.9--14.2~s). By contrast, restoring KV states takes 256.0~ms (2-chunk cold mean) to $<$350~ms (4-chunk, 37.45~MB) on UFS~2.2 and 48.0--56.2~ms on UFS~4.0---yielding a $155{:}1\text{--}212{:}1$ advantage on Device~A and $204{:}1\text{--}229{:}1$ on Device~B. Unlike datacenter GPUs with high-bandwidth HBM, mobile CPUs are memory-bandwidth bound, turning sequential flash reads into a highly favorable compute substitute.

\textbf{Memory footprint and thermal behavior.} Loading the 1.708~GB model and KV arena yielded an application PSS of 2.28~GB (2,280,940~KB) on Device~A and 1,986--2,176~MB on Device~B. Bounding $n_{\text{ctx}}\le2048$ via the JNI guard prevented out-of-memory terminations across all runs. During back-to-back baseline prefill runs on Device~A, battery temperatures rose from 29.5$^\circ$C to 38.2$^\circ$C, inducing DVFS throttling to 1.6~GHz; eliminating up to 86.67\% of prefill compute (47.3~s saved) directly reduces sustained CPU residency and thermal buildup.

\textbf{Threats to validity.} Background Android services can cause I/O contention (as isolated in Case~3); we mitigated this via randomized pair ordering, arena flushes, \texttt{/proc/\allowbreak\{PID\}/\allowbreak io} tracking, and 95\% bootstrap CIs. Externally, our scaling matrix evaluates a single multi-hop prompt structure across three prefix lengths ($K\in\{1,2,4\}$) on Gemma~2~2B Q4\_K\_M and two Snapdragon SoCs; NPU execution or non-transformer state-space models may exhibit different trade-offs.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\looseness=-1
We presented \systemname{}, an evidence-version-aware KV-cache framework for on-device RAG that binds cached prefixes to hierarchical SHA-256 digests under a canonical layout, repairs mutated downstream chunks via prefix invalidation, and enforces JNI context-budget guards. Across mid-range and flagship Android smartphones, \systemname{} reduces prefill compute by up to 86.67\% and 86.20\% ($3.65\times$ wall speedup) with zero truncation, 100\% multi-hop recall, and complete immunity to stale-cache hallucinations. Two-way ANOVAs confirm that cached prefix tokens govern savings ($p<10^{-15}$) independently of context capacity ($p=0.650\text{--}0.981$).
