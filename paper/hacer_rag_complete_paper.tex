\begin{abstract}
On-device Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) offers private, low-latency question answering, but small language models (SLMs) on mobile CPUs pay a severe prompt-prefill cost whenever retrieved context is re-encoded. Restored Key-Value (KV) caches remove this cost, yet mobile runtimes treat cache files as opaque snapshots, causing \emph{silent stale-cache hallucination} when evidence changes and exposing context overflows and continuation-boundary errors. We present \systemname{} (\emph{Hierarchical Adaptive Cache, Evidence-version-aware Repair for on-device RAG}), which binds every KV segment to SHA-256 chunk hashes under a canonical evidence layout, re-prefills only changed downstream chunks, falls back to scratch prefill on corruption, and enforces a JNI-level context-budget guard. We evaluate 4-bit Gemma~2~2B via \texttt{llama.rn}/\allowbreak\texttt{llama.cpp} across two physical Android tiers: mid-range (Snapdragon 4 Gen 2, UFS 2.2) and flagship (Snapdragon 8 Elite, UFS 4.0). Across 90 matched pairs and nine configurations ($K\in\{1,2,4\}$ chunks, $n_{\text{ctx}}\in\{512,1024,2048\}$), the mid-range device achieves prefill compute reductions of 19.34--21.21\%, 38.53--40.37\% and 86.06--86.67\%, saving up to 47.33~s ($3.65\times$ wall speedup) with zero truncation and 100\% deterministic output parity. The flagship shows identical scaling (16.35\%, 36.73\%, 85.23\%; 1.08~pp from Device~A at $K{=}4$) despite a $4.04\times$ raw compute advantage. Balanced Two-Way ANOVAs show reductions are governed by cached tokens ($p<10^{-15}$), not context capacity ($p=0.650\text{--}0.981$) or interaction ($p>0.72$). UFS~4.0 restores a 4-chunk session in 48.0--56.2~ms, saving 10.8--11.5~s ($204\times$--$229\times$). Multi-hop recall stays at 100\%, and across five dynamic-corpus failure scenarios \systemname{} eliminates the stale hallucinations and crashes where naive session reuse fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
On-device RAG offers privacy, offline capability and predictable availability~\cite{lewis2020retrieval,wu2024mobilellm}: a local retriever (e.g., SQLite BM25 or a mobile vector index) selects evidence chunks, builds an augmented prompt and feeds it to an on-device SLM~\cite{guu2020realm,liu2026percache}. Mobile hardware is tightly constrained~\cite{alizadeh2024llmflash,xue2024edgmoe}. Budget handsets pair heterogeneous SoCs (Snapdragon 4 Gen 2: $2\times$A78 + $6\times$A55) with 4--6~GB LPDDR4X~\cite{reddi2021mlperfmobile}, and even flagships (Snapdragon 8 Elite, 12~GB LPDDR5X) face thermal and battery envelopes. For 4-bit models~\cite{dettmers2022llmint8,frantar2022gptq} such as Gemma~2~2B Q4\_K\_M~\cite{gemma2024gemma2}, \emph{decode} runs at 20--30~tok/s on budget and 60--80~tok/s on flagship cores (memory-bandwidth bound), while \emph{prefill} of a $\sim$400-token prompt takes 50--65~s on mid-range and 12--14~s on flagship CPUs.

Because users query the same corpus repeatedly, the retriever returns identical or overlapping chunks~\cite{lu2024turborag,liu2026percache}, yet a conventional runtime re-encodes them every time, draining the battery and inducing throttling. Caching KV tensors is the natural fix. On Device~A, prefilling a 395-token prompt (4 threads) takes 54.2~s (7.3~tok/s) whereas reading a 40~MB KV cache from UFS~2.2 takes under 350~ms; on Device~B (UFS~4.0) prefill takes 13.1~s versus 48.0~ms to load. Prefill is thus more than $150\times$ slower than flash restoration.

However, runtimes such as \texttt{llama.rn} and \texttt{llama.cpp}~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp} expose \texttt{saveSession}/\allowbreak\texttt{loadSession} as file-level snapshots meant for conversational resumption: a cache is tied to a path or ID with no check of semantic validity. Applied blindly to RAG this exposes three failures:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Silent stale-KV hallucination.} If a document is edited or revoked (a policy, medical record or price list), restoring the old session makes the model attend to obsolete KV tensors and emit outdated facts with high confidence~\cite{ji2023hallucination}.
\item \textbf{Context headroom violations.} Runtimes allocate a fixed KV arena of $2LHD\,n_{\text{ctx}}$ elements ($L$ layers, $H$ heads, head dimension $D$), so developers choose small $n_{\text{ctx}}$ (512/1024). \texttt{llama.cpp} also reserves 4 internal tokens; if prefix, query and generation budget exceed the limit, leading context is silently dropped, corrupting the attention mask. Oversized arenas risk Android Low Memory Killer (LMK) termination.
\item \textbf{Continuation boundary misalignment.} Appending query text to a restored prefix without modeling tokenizer boundary merging causes index misalignment, garbled output or redundant re-evaluation.
\end{itemize}
On 4~GB phones, caches must be persisted to flash between interactions, which makes validity checking essential.

\textbf{Research questions.} \textbf{RQ1} (scaling and context independence): how does prefill reduction scale with cached prefix tokens on budget and flagship tiers, and does allocated $n_{\text{ctx}}$ have a significant effect? \textbf{RQ2} (freshness): can lightweight cryptographic evidence versioning eliminate stale-KV hallucination under updates, revocations and reorderings without full scratch prefill? \textbf{RQ3} (multi-hop integrity): does partial prefix reuse preserve factual accuracy when answers need synthesis across several retrieved passages? \textbf{RQ4} (I/O trade-off): what is the latency trade-off between reading KV caches from UFS~2.2/4.0 flash and native CPU prefill?

\textbf{Contributions.} (1)~The \systemname{} architecture: canonical layout, SHA-256 hashing, context-budget safety and selective repair. (2)~A 90-pair scaling benchmark over nine configurations: 19.3--86.7\% prefill reduction, up to $3.65\times$ wall speedup. (3)~Generalization to a flagship phone: $4.04\times$ raw speedup, identical relative scaling. (4)~Two-way ANOVA on both platforms: cached tokens, not context capacity, drive reduction ($p<10^{-15}$). (5)~Multi-chunk integrity: 44.13\% mean reduction, $1.63\times$ wall speedup, 100\% multi-hop recall, failing incomplete-evidence ablations. (6)~A five-scenario staleness suite where naive reuse hallucinates or crashes and \systemname{} does not.

\textbf{Non-claims.} We do not claim that saving and restoring KV caches is novel; our contribution is evidence-version-aware binding, selective repair, context-budget safety and mobile systems characterization. We claim no universal law across transformer architectures or SoCs: all results are bounded to the two phones, \texttt{llama.rn} v0.5.8/\texttt{llama.cpp} and Gemma~2~2B Instruct Q4\_K\_M. The five scenarios are proof-of-concept stress tests, not a proof of correctness for arbitrary document edits.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
\textbf{KV-cache fundamentals.} In a decoder-only transformer, token $t$ attends only to positions $\tau\le t$ through a causal mask. The Key/Value tensors of any prefix of length $P$ therefore do not depend on later tokens and can be reused without re-evaluation, provided model weights and positional encodings~\cite{su2024roformer} are unchanged. \texttt{llama.cpp}~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp} serializes the KV arena and the evaluated token sequence; restoring a session lets prefill of the continuation start at position $P{+}1$.

\textbf{KV reuse in RAG.} \emph{TurboRAG}~\cite{lu2024turborag} precomputes document caches offline but needs positional transformations and optional fine-tuning. \emph{KVLink}~\cite{yang2025kvlink} adds trainable linking tokens and modifies the model, which is incompatible with off-the-shelf quantized models. \emph{PerCache}~\cite{liu2026percache} pre-loads user profiles but does not address cryptographic versioning, revocation or multi-chunk repair. Server engines (vLLM~\cite{kwon2023vllm}, SGLang) share prefixes in GPU memory and ignore cold flash I/O, thermal envelopes and Android memory eviction. KV compression such as KIVI~\cite{liu2024kivi} and H$_2$O~\cite{zhang2024h2o} shrinks memory but neither removes prefill for new documents nor detects staleness.

\textbf{Differentiation.} \systemname{} contributes (i)~evidence-version-aware binding of each persistent KV segment to SHA-256 hashes of its chunks, (ii)~a selective repair policy that invalidates affected segments and re-prefills minimally while keeping valid prefix state, (iii)~JNI-level context-budget and boundary-token safety, and (iv)~a rigorous flash-versus-compute characterization across two device tiers.

\systemname{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}) has five subsystems.

\textbf{Evidence ingestion and versioning.} Documents are split into chunks $\mathcal{C}=\{C_1,\dots,C_M\}$, each stored with a digest $H(C_i)=\text{SHA-256}(\text{Canonicalize}(C_i))$, creation timestamp, document ID and token count in SQLite. Any edit, deletion or insertion changes $H(C_i)$.

\textbf{Canonical evidence layout.} Retrievers rank chunks by score, so small fluctuations return permutations ($[C_2,C_1]$ vs.\ $[C_1,C_2]$). Under causal attention a permutation changes every token and voids any saved prefix (0\% reuse). \systemname{} fixes the order deterministically, $\pi_{\text{canon}}=\text{Sort}(\{C_1,\dots,C_k\})$ by persistent document and chunk IDs, so prefixes remain stable regardless of ranking noise.

\textbf{Adaptive cache decision engine.} For canonical chunks the engine forms $P=\text{FormatPrompt}(\pi_{\text{canon}}(\cdot))$ and key $\mathcal{K}_{\text{session}}=\text{SHA-256}(P)$ and inspects local flash:
\emph{Case~1 (exact hit):} a valid session matches and all $H(C_i)$ agree; only $Q$ is prefilled. \emph{Case~2 (selective repair):} chunks $C_1..C_j$ are unchanged, so the prefix KV is loaded and only $C_{j+1}..C_k$ and $Q$ are prefilled. \emph{Case~3 (safe invalidation):} a revoked, missing, corrupt or malformed file invalidates the session, purges orphans and restarts from position~0 (Algorithm~\ref{alg:hacer_repair}).

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{HACER cache validation and repair}
\label{alg:hacer_repair}
\scriptsize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE chunks $\mathcal{C}=[C_1..C_k]$, query $Q$, registry $\mathcal{R}$
\STATE $\mathcal{C}_c\gets\text{CanonicalSort}(\mathcal{C})$; $P\gets\text{FormatPrefix}(\mathcal{C}_c)$; $\mathcal{K}\gets\text{SHA-256}(P)$
\IF{$\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{R}$ \AND VerifyHashes$(\mathcal{C}_c,\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}])$ \AND LoadSession$(\mathcal{R}[\mathcal{K}])$}
  \STATE \COMMENT{Case 1} PrefillOnline$(Q)$; \textbf{return}
\ENDIF
\STATE $j^{*}\gets$ largest $j\in[0,k{-}1]$ with SHA-256(FormatPrefix$(\mathcal{C}_c[1{:}j])$)$\in\mathcal{R}$
\IF{$j^{*}>0$ \AND LoadSession(prefix $j^{*}$)}
  \STATE \COMMENT{Case 2} PrefillOnline(Tokenize$(\mathcal{C}_c[j^{*}{+}1{:}k]\Vert Q)$); SaveSession$(\mathcal{K})$; \textbf{return}
\ENDIF
\STATE \COMMENT{Case 3} PurgeInvalidSessions; ResetContextArena; PrefillOnline(Tokenize$(P\Vert Q)$); SaveSession$(\mathcal{K})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Native execution and telemetry.} The layer wraps \texttt{llama.rn} v0.5.8 and records kernel I/O counters from \texttt{/proc/\allowbreak\{PID\}/\allowbreak io} (separating flash reads from page-cache hits), Android PSS from \texttt{dumpsys meminfo} (LMK risk), and battery temperature and CPU frequencies from sysfs (thermal throttling).

\textbf{Blind reuse versus HACER.} Blind reuse (\texttt{llama\us load\us session\us file}) treats \texttt{session.bin} as authoritative without checking the documents behind it, so reordered chunks give 0\% reuse, changed or revoked documents yield silent stale answers, overflows truncate or trigger LMK kills, and boundary tokens merge uncontrolled. HACER instead sorts by persistent IDs, verifies SHA-256 chunk hashes, applies the JNI headroom test before inference, accounts for the $+1$ boundary token and separates flash reads from page-cache hits with \texttt{/proc/\allowbreak\{PID\}/\allowbreak io}.

\section{Context-Budget Safety and Boundary Alignment}
\label{sec:context_safety}
\textbf{Headroom guard.} Initializing $n_{\text{ctx}}\ge4096$ can reserve over 1.2~GB of contiguous KV memory and trigger LMKD termination on 4~GB devices. Before inference the JNI guard enforces
\begin{equation}
(n_{\text{ctx}}-\Delta_{\text{res}})-(N_{\text{prompt}}+N_{\text{predict}})\ge 0,
\label{eq:headroom}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_{\text{res}}=4$ tokens are reserved by \texttt{llama.cpp} for sequence demarcation and end-of-turn markers. On violation the request is rejected at the React Native bridge instead of being silently truncated. With $N_{\text{prompt}}=395$ and $N_{\text{predict}}=35$ the headroom is 78, 590 and 1614 tokens for $n_{\text{ctx}}=512$, 1024 and 2048. Across all 182 recorded executions the reported headroom matched this budget exactly, with zero engine truncations (\texttt{truncated==0}).

\textbf{Boundary-token invariant.} Naive code assumes a session restored with $N_{\text{loaded}}$ tokens leaves $N_{\text{prompt}}-N_{\text{loaded}}$ tokens to evaluate. Tracing \texttt{llama.rn} and \texttt{llama.cpp} shows otherwise: because BPE merges leading whitespace or delimiters with preceding context, prefill restarts at index $N_{\text{loaded}}-1$ so that the first new token sees full cross-boundary context. Hence for $N_{\text{loaded}}>0$
\begin{equation}
N_{\text{eval}}=N_{\text{prompt}}-N_{\text{loaded}}+1,
\label{eq:boundary_token}
\end{equation}
and $N_{\text{eval}}=N_{\text{prompt}}=395$ for a cold start. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:boundary_token} gives $N_{\text{eval}}=312$, $238$ and $55$ for $K{=}1,2,4$ ($N_{\text{loaded}}=84,158,341$). Ignoring the $+1$ yields off-by-one prefill errors, corrupted positions or wrong compute attribution.

\textbf{Software and model.} A React Native app (\texttt{com.chat}) calls \texttt{llama.rn} v0.5.8, which compiles \texttt{llama.cpp} with ARM NEON and OpenMP. The model is Gemma~2~2B Instruct~\cite{gemma2024gemma2} in GGUF \texttt{Q4\_K\_M} ($\sim$1.708~GB; SHA-256 \texttt{e0aee85060f168f0\ldots}). Decoding is greedy (temperature 0) with $N_{\text{predict}}=35$ for strict determinism.

\textbf{Workload.} A synthetic enterprise corpus (Zephyr Dynamics) yields a canonical prompt of four evidence chunks plus a multi-hop question: Chunk~1 company overview (founded 2011, 847 employees); Chunk~2 leadership (CEO Priya Nandakumar, CLO Jorge Fonseca, CTO Marcus L{\"o}bell, CFO Saoirse Hennessey); Chunk~3 AeroSense X7 sensor (12,000~m altitude, 72~h battery); Chunk~4 SwarmKit Pro (2,048 drones, \$1,200 standard and \$3,800 full-capacity tiers). The question asks the founding year, CLO, battery life and full-capacity price. The prompt tokenizes to exactly $N_{\text{prompt}}=395$ tokens with SHA-256 digest \texttt{a5f1db09b904e813\ldots27c6d}, identical in every execution.

\textbf{Protocol.} (i)~Each baseline run is preceded by flushing the context arena with an orthogonal conversational prompt so no KV tokens linger. (ii)~Order within each pair (baseline-first or reuse-first) is randomized to avoid bias from heating or background tasks. (iii)~Per run we record native prefill time (\texttt{prompt\us ms}) and decode time (\texttt{predicted\us ms}) from \texttt{llama.cpp} timers, end-to-end wall time at the bridge, \texttt{/proc/\allowbreak\{PID\}/\allowbreak io} deltas, PSS, and the generated text for byte-level validation.

\textbf{Systems compared.} (a)~\emph{Full prefill}: the whole prompt is evaluated in a fresh arena ($N_{\text{loaded}}=0$). (b)~\emph{Ordinary blind session reuse}: \texttt{llama.rn} session restore by fixed file name. (c)~\emph{HACER}: canonical layout, SHA-256 verification, JNI budget enforcement, boundary alignment and selective repair.

\textbf{Metrics.} Native prefill latency $T_{\text{prefill}}$ (s); prefill reduction and wall speedup
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{prefill}}=\frac{T^{\text{base}}_{\text{prefill}}-T^{\text{reuse}}_{\text{prefill}}}{T^{\text{base}}_{\text{prefill}}}\times100\%,\quad
S_{\text{wall}}=\frac{T^{\text{base}}_{\text{wall}}}{T^{\text{reuse}}_{\text{wall}}},
\end{equation}
with 95\% CIs from 2,000-iteration non-parametric bootstrap; flash cache-load overhead $T_{\text{load}}$ (ms); storage read volume, where runs reading $\ge85\%$ of the session size from flash are \emph{cold flash reads} and those served $\ge85\%$ by the kernel buffer cache are \emph{warm page-cache hits}; multi-hop factual recall (\%)~\cite{min2023factscore}; and stale-fact hallucination rate (\%) after document updates.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{Storage--compute asymmetry.} Dense CPU prefill reaches only 7.29~tok/s on Snapdragon 4 Gen 2 (54.2~s for 395 tokens) and 28.4~tok/s on Snapdragon 8 Elite (11.9--14.2~s), whereas a 37--40~MB KV binary loads in 256.0~ms (UFS 2.2) or 48.0--56.2~ms (UFS 4.0). The asymmetry exceeds $150{:}1$ on Device~A and $204{:}1$--$229{:}1$ on Device~B. Unlike servers with H100-class GPUs prefilling thousands of tokens per second, mobile platforms invert the trade-off, and flash becomes a fast, energy-efficient cache for expensive computation.

\textbf{Memory pressure.} On the 4~GB device the OS and services use 1.8--2.2~GB, and \texttt{com.chat} reached PSS of about 2.28~GB (2,280,940~KB; Device~A) and 1,986--2,176~MB (Device~B) with the 1.7~GB weights and arena. Raising $n_{\text{ctx}}$ to 4096 would trigger an LMK SIGKILL, so JNI budgeting bounds operation to $n_{\text{ctx}}\le2048$ ($n_{\text{ctx}}\in\{512,1024,2048\}$); KV state lives on flash and the arena is released between interactions.

\textbf{Thermal and energy.} In continuous Device~A runs battery temperature rose from 29.5$^\circ$C to 38.2$^\circ$C and DVFS throttled clocks to 1.6~GHz. Eliminating up to 86.67\% of prefill (46.7--47.3~s on A, 10.8--11.5~s on B) shortens high-power CPU residency, conserving battery and keeping decode responsive.

\textbf{Implications.} (1)~Evidence binding is mandatory when local documents change; hashing adds only microseconds. (2)~Canonical layout removes the permutation penalty. (3)~Selective repair keeps most savings while guaranteeing freshness. (4)~Flagship hardware still needs prefill optimization: cutting 13~s to 1.8~s separates unacceptable lag from fluid conversation.

\textbf{Threats to internal validity.} Commercial Android hosts run non-deterministic background daemons, as the Case~3 anomaly showed. We mitigated this with matched pairs, randomized order, context flushing, kernel I/O logging, bootstrap CIs, and interleaved trials with \texttt{dumpsys battery} logging against junction-temperature throttling. The two quarantined trials were logged with full telemetry before analysis.

\textbf{Threats to external validity.} Only two phones (mid-range and flagship) were tested; SoCs with NPUs (Hexagon, MediaTek APU) or eMMC~5.1 storage, and other memory or thermal profiles, may differ. Only Gemma~2~2B Q4\_K\_M (interleaved local/global attention, GQA, RoPE) was evaluated; sliding-window-only or state-space models (e.g., Mamba) may have different prefix-caching trade-offs. The five dynamic scenarios are targeted stress tests, not an exhaustive survey of document mutation patterns.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
On-device RAG is bottlenecked by prompt prefill on low-power CPUs, and existing mobile runtimes expose KV reuse only as opaque session files that invite stale hallucination, context overflow and boundary errors. \systemname{} binds persistent KV tensors to document versions through a canonical layout and SHA-256 chunk hashing, repairs only changed downstream chunks, and enforces a JNI-level budget guard. On two Android tiers it reduced prefill by up to 86.67\% and 86.20\%, with 100\% output parity and zero truncation. Cached tokens, not context capacity, govern savings ($p<10^{-15}$ vs.\ $p=0.650\text{--}0.981$); multi-hop recall stays at 100\%; and all five dynamic scenarios are handled where naive reuse fails. Evidence-version-aware KV reuse is thus a practical foundation for fast, private and fresh on-device conversational AI.
